# Working AI Hypotheses

These are hypotheses to test, not conclusions.

## Phonetics

- AI will perform better on high-resource orthographic transcription than on defensible narrow phonetic transcription.
- Models may generate plausible-looking IPA that reflects spelling or standard-language descriptions rather than the supplied speaker's actual signal.
- Automated acoustic measurement may save time but requires human inspection of segmentation and tracking errors.

## Low-resource relevance

- AI assistance becomes less reliable as training-data coverage, standardized orthography, and published grammatical description decrease.
- Human/native-speaker feedback becomes more important, not less, when model confidence exceeds available evidence.

## Bible translation

- The safest high-value uses may initially be consistency checks, retrieval, corpus alignment, draft comparison, terminology tracking, and anomaly detection rather than unsupervised final translation.
- Linguistically trained humans are necessary to know what evidence the model is missing.

Update these statements only when experiments or strong external research justify it.
