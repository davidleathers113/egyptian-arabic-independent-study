# Credit-Hour and Workload Methodology

## Planning convention

For this project, **1 modeled semester credit = 45 documented hours of academic engagement**.

Therefore:
- 3-credit study → 135 hours;
- 4-credit study → 180 hours;
- 13-credit sequence → 585 hours.

This is a curriculum-design convention, **not a claim of institutional credit**.

## Why 45 hours?

Federal credit-hour guidance uses a traditional reference point of roughly one hour of instruction plus at least two hours of out-of-class work each week for approximately 15 weeks per semester credit, while allowing equivalent work in other formats. That creates a useful conservative planning target for a self-directed course.

Source: https://www.ed.gov/laws-and-policy/higher-education-laws-and-policy/program-integrity-information-questions-and-12

## What counts

Count academically purposeful work tied to an outcome:
- assigned reading and reading responses;
- structured lectures;
- IPA drills and problem sets;
- coach/consultant elicitation sessions;
- transcription and analysis;
- Praat labs;
- writing and revision;
- project work;
- assessments and remediation;
- teach-back preparation/recording;
- documented AI experiments.

Do not count passive commuting, background listening unrelated to an assignment, administrative file organization beyond reasonable project work, or ordinary Arabic practice unless explicitly part of a course activity.

## Documentation rule

Every logged session records date, activity, duration, linked course outcome, and artifact/result when applicable. Hours are a constraint; demonstrated competence remains the real goal.
