# AL4410 Learning Outcomes

By the end of the study, the student will be able to:

1. Segment unfamiliar forms into plausible morphs and defend the segmentation.
2. Distinguish morpheme, morph, allomorph, and suppletion in analysis.
3. Determine word classes from language-specific morphological and syntactic behavior rather than English labels alone.
4. Identify and explain phonologically, lexically, and morphologically conditioned allomorphy.
5. Analyze inflectional systems including agreement, person/number/gender, case, tense/aspect/mood where present.
6. Analyze derivational morphology and changes in category/meaning/valence.
7. Recognize and analyze nonconcatenative morphology, including Arabic root-and-pattern data, without assuming every Arabic pattern has a simple morpheme-to-string mapping.
8. Distinguish words, clitics, affixes, and uncertain boundary cases using multiple diagnostics.
9. Identify constituents using distributional and structural evidence.
10. Analyze noun phrases, verb phrases, adpositional/oblique structures, and other language-specific phrase types.
11. Distinguish semantic roles from grammatical relations.
12. Analyze argument structure, valence, and selected valence-changing/voice constructions.
13. Describe basic clause patterns and constituent-order variation.
14. Analyze negation, questions, commands, coordination, subordination, embedded clauses, and relative clauses at an introductory level.
15. Elicit paradigms and sentence sets from the Egyptian Arabic consultant to discriminate competing grammatical hypotheses.
16. Produce interlinearized examples with consistent morpheme segmentation and glossing conventions.
17. Write a coherent preliminary grammar sketch whose claims are linked to data and whose uncertainties remain explicit.
18. Critically evaluate AI-generated morphological/syntactic analyses using human-derived evidence and cross-linguistic reasoning.

## Evidence requirement

Every mastered outcome should be traceable to at least one graded artifact or assessment. The final grammar sketch is synthesis evidence, not a substitute for cross-linguistic problem-solving competence.
