# Project — AI Grammar-Induction Benchmark

## Research question

How reliably can current AI systems infer basic morphology and syntax from finite, low-resource-style datasets, and what evidence do they expose for their conclusions?

## Dataset mix

Use 8–12 datasets spanning:
- morpheme segmentation/allomorphy;
- inflection;
- derivation;
- nonlinear morphology;
- word classes;
- constituency;
- grammatical relations/valence;
- clause structure;
- complex sentences.

At least half must be non-Arabic and should represent different typological profiles.

## Procedure

1. Human analysis without AI.
2. Freeze response.
3. Grade against authoritative source/key where available.
4. Give identical evidence and task definition to AI.
5. Preserve AI response.
6. Score using the same rubric.
7. Test whether asking the AI to cite specific data improves reliability.

## Failure taxonomy

Track:
- invented morphemes or meanings;
- segmentation forced by familiar-language assumptions;
- category labels without diagnostics;
- confusion of semantic role and grammatical relation;
- ignored counterexamples;
- overconfident claims from sparse data;
- inability to represent competing analyses;
- errors caused by orthography;
- correct pattern detection with incorrect explanation.

## Egyptian Arabic extension

Select a few original paradigms/constructions and compare:
- student analysis;
- AI analysis;
- published description after freeze;
- follow-up consultant evidence.

## Final report

3,000–5,000 words plus scoring tables. Conclude with implications for AI-assisted linguistic analysis and Bible translation: where AI might accelerate paradigm discovery, consistency checks, hypothesis generation, or corpus search, and what human/native-speaker validation remains essential.
