# Egyptian Arabic Morphology/Syntax — Post-Analysis Reading Sequence

## Rule

These are **comparison readings**, not answer keys.

For every matching topic:
1. collect/organize speaker/corpus data;
2. analyze independently;
3. freeze the analysis with confidence ratings;
4. read the source;
5. record agreement/disagreement;
6. revise only with a written evidence-based rationale.

---

## General reference — use throughout after independent analysis

### Abdel-Massih, Ernest T.; Abdel-Malek, Zaki N.; Badawi, El-Said M.; with Ernest N. McCarus. _A Reference Grammar of Egyptian Arabic_. Georgetown University Press, 2009 reprint of the 1979 work.

Role:
- lookup after analysis of grammatical phenomena;
- terminology/example comparison;
- checking whether a phenomenon is already described;
- identifying additional paradigms/constructions to test with the consultant.

Because the book is arranged alphabetically by grammatical term rather than as a course, it is especially well suited to post-analysis consultation.

---

## Week 4 — Verb inflection / tense-aspect morphology

### Elabbas Benmamoun — “The Tense Systems of Egyptian Arabic, Moroccan Arabic, and Standard Arabic” in _The Feature Structure of Functional Categories_ (Oxford University Press, 2000).

Read only after building your own controlled Egyptian Arabic verb paradigms.

Questions:
- What forms/functions did you identify independently?
- Does the source separate morphology from syntax differently?
- Which labels are descriptive and which depend on a formal framework?
- Do your consultant's forms match the variety represented in the source?

Do not make formal TP analysis an AL4410 mastery requirement.

---

## Weeks 5–6 — Derivation and nonlinear morphology

### _A Reference Grammar of Egyptian Arabic_
Consult entries relevant to verb forms, verbal nouns, participles, plurals, derivation, and other patterns actually observed in your data.

### Optional computational comparator
CAMeL Tools Egyptian Arabic morphology database/analyzer (`calima-egy-r13`).

Compare only after manual paradigms are established.

Audit:
- proposed segmentation;
- roots/stems;
- grammatical features;
- ambiguous analyses;
- forms the analyzer fails to cover;
- differences between analyzer conventions and your descriptive goals.

---

## Week 7 — Clitics and word division

Use the reference grammar plus CAMeL Tools tokenization only after producing an independent segmentation analysis.

Ask:
- Is an element being treated as an affix, clitic, or orthographic attachment?
- What evidence supports that label?
- Does automated tokenization encode a corpus convention rather than a claim about grammatical wordhood?

This distinction should be explicit in the portfolio.

---

## Week 10 — Agreement / grammatical relations / clause structure

### Aoun, Joseph E.; Benmamoun, Elabbas; Choueiri, Lina. _The Syntax of Arabic_. Cambridge University Press.

Potential post-analysis chapters:
- clause structure;
- syntax of subjects;
- sentential agreement.

This is a graduate-level/formal syntax comparator. Extract descriptive facts and competing analyses without making the formal theory a prerequisite-course requirement.

---

## Week 13 — Sentential negation

### Usama Soltan — “The morphosyntax of sentential negation in Cairene Egyptian Arabic revisited: Splitting Neg” (2014).

Read after assembling your own affirmative/negative clause contrasts across relevant predicates/TAM contexts.

Compare:
- forms and distribution of negation;
- morphological vs. syntactic observations;
- contexts that challenge a single simple rule;
- how much of the author's conclusion depends on formal syntactic assumptions.

Your AL4410 deliverable is a careful descriptive account, not replication of the paper's theoretical structure.

---

## Week 13 — Questions

### Aoun, Benmamoun & Choueiri — chapter on modes of wh-interrogation.

Read after collecting Egyptian Arabic question data.

Focus on descriptive contrasts:
- wh expression position;
- gaps/resumptive elements where relevant;
- clause structure differences;
- restrictions observed.

Defer advanced movement/island theory if it exceeds the course goal.

---

## Week 14 — Relative clauses

### Aoun, Benmamoun & Choueiri — chapter on restrictive relatives.

Read after independently analyzing representative relative constructions.

Focus on:
- relative markers;
- antecedent properties;
- gaps/resumptives;
- clause-internal structure;
- variety differences.

Again, the independent study requires descriptive analysis, not graduate-level formal derivation.

---

## Optional later reading — left periphery / focus / correlation

Do not make these core AL4410 topics unless the corpus naturally raises them.

Possible advanced references:
- Aoun, Benmamoun & Choueiri on clitic-left dislocation/focus and left periphery;
- Usama Soltan on Egyptian Arabic correlation structures.

These may be useful bridges to AL5313 or AL5312 rather than prerequisite requirements.
