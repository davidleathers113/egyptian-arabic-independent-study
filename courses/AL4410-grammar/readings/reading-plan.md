# AL4410 Detailed Reading and Resource Plan

## How to use this plan

This course is not built around reading several grammar books cover to cover. Each source has a distinct job:

- **Bickford** = primary field-oriented analytical procedure and grammar-sketch spine.
- **Payne, _Exploring Language Structure_** = second explanation plus extensive cross-linguistic exercises.
- **Lieber** = selective modern morphology deepening.
- **Essentials of Linguistics** = free review/self-check.
- **WALS/Grambank** = post-analysis typological comparison and hypothesis challenging.
- **MIT OCW** = selected practice/stretch work; 24.902 is intentionally not the backbone.
- **Leipzig Glossing Rules** = presentation standard.
- **Egyptian Arabic scholarship** = post-analysis comparison only.

Do not invent page numbers across editions. Record the exact pages from the copies actually used before beginning a week.

# Week-by-week assignments

## Week 1 — Grammatical analysis from data

### Required
- Bickford Chs. 1–4, selectively but substantially.
- Payne Ch. 1.
- Leipzig Glossing Rules: full initial read.
- Essentials of Linguistics morphology introduction for review.

### Practice
- segment unfamiliar-language word sets;
- distinguish observations from hypotheses;
- produce consistent three-/four-line interlinear examples;
- start the Egyptian Arabic paradigm/analysis notebook.

### Rule
No Egyptian Arabic reference grammar lookup for forms chosen as baseline analysis data.

---

## Week 2 — Word classes and language-specific diagnostics

### Required
- Bickford Ch. 5 sections on syntactic categories; revisit Ch. 2 terminology.
- Payne Ch. 4 and selected Ch. 5 material.
- Lieber Ch. 1 selectively.

### Practice
- diagnose noun/verb/adjective-like categories using distribution and morphology rather than English translation;
- analyze at least two datasets where familiar semantic labels are misleading.

### Typology check — after analysis
Use Grambank selectively to inspect how languages vary in major lexical-class patterns. Do not use database labels as the argument for your analysis.

---

## Week 3 — Allomorphy, suppletion, and morphophonemic boundaries

### Required
- Bickford Ch. 12 plus relevant Ch. 3 hypothesis procedure.
- Payne Ch. 3.
- Lieber Ch. 9 selectively.

### Practice
- distinguish phonologically predictable allomorphy from lexically/morphologically conditioned patterns;
- carry forward relevant AL4303 evidence without re-solving phonology from scratch;
- build Egyptian Arabic mini-paradigms only from independently collected/verified forms.

---

## Week 4 — Inflection, agreement, case, and grammatical categories

### Required
- Bickford Ch. 10 and first pass through Ch. 19.
- Lieber Ch. 6 and selected Ch. 7.
- Payne Ch. 2 where conceptual categories overlap with morphological expression.

### Practice
- identify person/number/gender, tense/aspect/mood, case, agreement, or analogous categories in unfamiliar systems;
- distinguish one form expressing multiple features from multiple morphemes expressing separate features;
- compare head vs. dependent marking after human analysis using WALS Ch. 23 and related maps.

### Egyptian Arabic post-analysis reading
After independently building a controlled verb/TAM paradigm, consult:
- Abdel-Massih, Abdel-Malek & Badawi, _A Reference Grammar of Egyptian Arabic_, relevant entries;
- Benmamoun, chapter on tense systems of Egyptian Arabic/Moroccan Arabic/Standard Arabic, selectively.

The goal is comparison, not importing the authors' analysis as your starting point.

---

## Week 5 — Derivation, valence, and morphology-syntax interaction

### Required
- Bickford Ch. 11; first pass through Chs. 8 and 21.
- Payne Ch. 9.
- Lieber Ch. 3 and Ch. 8 selectively.

### Practice
- distinguish inflection from lexeme formation;
- analyze derivational patterns and resulting category/valence changes;
- work at least one causative/applicative/passive-like dataset from a non-Arabic language.

---

## Week 6 — Nonlinear morphology and Egyptian Arabic as a serious lab

### Required
- Bickford Ch. 13.
- Payne Ch. 2 sections on nonconcatenative processes if applicable.
- Lieber Ch. 5 and typological portions of Ch. 7 selectively.

### Egyptian Arabic project
Build a data-driven case study of root/pattern or other nonlinear morphology using actual paradigms. Do not assume every alternation is best modeled as a root-and-template operation simply because Arabic is Semitic.

### Optional computational comparison — only after manual analysis
Use CAMeL Tools' Egyptian Arabic `calima-egy-r13` morphology database/analyzer to compare:
- segmentation;
- root/stem labels;
- POS/features;
- alternative analyses;
- errors or ambiguities relative to your consultant/corpus data.

Document disagreements rather than treating the analyzer as ground truth.

---

## Week 7 — Wordhood, clitics, and segmentation

### Required
- Bickford Ch. 20.
- Revisit Payne Chs. 2–3 as needed.
- Leipzig Glossing Rules again, especially segmentation/gloss alignment.

### Practice
- distinguish orthographic words, phonological words, grammatical words, clitics, and affixes using explicit diagnostics;
- analyze at least one non-Arabic clitic/wordhood problem;
- audit Egyptian Arabic tokenization assumptions.

### Optional tool lab
After manual segmentation is reliable, experiment with SIL FieldWorks Language Explorer on a small approved text. FLEx can store lexemes, segment words into morphemes, suggest previously approved analyses, and generate interlinear output, but human approval remains authoritative.

---

## Week 8 — Morphology synthesis and midterm

### Required
- review Bickford Chs. 3–4, 10–13, 19–20;
- review Payne Chs. 2–5;
- no major new theoretical unit.

### Assessment
Closed-AI morphology midterm using unseen cross-linguistic datasets.

### Portfolio task
Create an error taxonomy: segmentation, category, allomorphy, feature analysis, inflection/derivation, wordhood, nonlinear morphology, evidence/overgeneralization.

---

## Week 9 — Constituency, phrases, and structure

### Required
- Bickford Chs. 5–6.
- Payne Ch. 6.
- Essentials of Linguistics syntax chapter for accessible review.

### Practice
- constituency diagnostics and hierarchical structure;
- head/complement/modifier distinctions at a descriptive level;
- phrase-structure hypotheses in unfamiliar data.

### Stretch
MIT 24.900 syntax lectures/problem material can supplement this week. MIT 24.902 material is optional and should be used only if it clarifies rather than displaces descriptive analysis.

---

## Week 10 — Grammatical relations, argument structure, obliques, case/agreement

### Required
- Bickford Chs. 8–9 and second pass through Ch. 19.
- Payne Ch. 8 and selected Ch. 9.
- WALS after analysis: selected maps on locus of marking, case, and related grammatical-relation evidence.

### Practice
- distinguish semantic roles from grammatical relations;
- avoid assuming every language has English-like subjects/objects diagnosed by the same tests;
- analyze transitive/intransitive/ditransitive and oblique patterns.

### Egyptian Arabic post-analysis comparison
Consult relevant entries in _A Reference Grammar of Egyptian Arabic_. For more formal/theoretical comparison, selected chapters of Aoun, Benmamoun & Choueiri's _The Syntax of Arabic_ may be read **after** the descriptive analysis is frozen.

---

## Week 11 — Noun phrase structure, possession, modification, agreement

### Required
- Bickford Ch. 7 and relevant Ch. 19 material.
- Payne Ch. 6 plus relevant word-class/subclass sections.
- WALS only after analysis for comparison of adjective/noun, genitive/noun, numeral/noun, adposition/NP orders.

### Practice
- build an Egyptian Arabic NP data set covering determiners/articles where relevant, adjectives/modifiers, possession, pronouns, numerals, agreement, and embedding;
- analyze at least one non-Arabic NP structure materially unlike English/Arabic.

---

## Week 12 — Constituent order and typology

### Required
- Bickford Ch. 15.
- Payne Ch. 7.
- WALS word-order chapters selectively after hypotheses are formed.
- Grambank for broader feature comparison after human analysis.

### Practice
- distinguish basic/dominant constituent order from discourse-driven variation;
- document evidence rather than assigning a language one rigid order too early;
- compare correlated orders cautiously.

### Boundary
Information structure itself belongs more fully to later discourse study; do not let this week become AL5312.

---

## Week 13 — Questions, commands, negation, and clause types

### Required
- Bickford Chs. 16–17.
- relevant Payne material from Chs. 6, 8, or 10 as needed.
- typological references only after analysis.

### Egyptian Arabic post-analysis readings
After collecting and freezing controlled examples:
- Usama Soltan, “The morphosyntax of sentential negation in Cairene Egyptian Arabic revisited: Splitting Neg” — as an advanced comparator, not the expected undergraduate analysis.
- selected Aoun, Benmamoun & Choueiri material on wh-interrogation if useful.

### Practice
Collect natural-context examples rather than translating an English questionnaire sentence-by-sentence.

---

## Week 14 — Multi-clause syntax: embedding, complementation, adverbials, relatives

### Required
- Bickford Chs. 22–23.
- Payne Ch. 10.

### Practice
- distinguish coordination/subordination;
- identify complement, adverbial, and relative-clause strategies;
- track overt markers, argument sharing, gaps/resumptives where relevant, and clause-internal morphology;
- analyze one unfamiliar non-Arabic multi-clause system.

### Egyptian Arabic post-analysis comparison
Use:
- _A Reference Grammar of Egyptian Arabic_, relevant entries;
- Aoun, Benmamoun & Choueiri, restrictive-relative chapter, selectively and after your own data analysis.

Do not require graduate movement/resumption theory for mastery.

---

## Week 15 — Syntax synthesis + AI/automation benchmark

### Required
- targeted review of weak Bickford/Payne chapters.
- no new broad theoretical unit.

### Assessment
Closed-AI unseen syntax practical.

### AI benchmark
After human grading, test selected data with:
- a general LLM;
- CAMeL Tools for Egyptian morphological analysis where applicable;
- optionally another structured parser/glosser.

Evaluate segmentation, glossing, feature assignment, constituency/relations, unsupported certainty, missed ambiguity, and evidence traceability.

For research context, consult recent ACL work on automatic interlinear glossing only **after** your benchmark design is frozen.

---

## Week 16 — Grammar sketch, writing, evidence audit, oral defense

### Required
- Bickford Chs. 18, 24, and 25; consult 26–27 as organizational aids.
- review Leipzig Glossing Rules.
- selected Payne chapters needed for unresolved descriptions.

### Outputs
- preliminary Egyptian Arabic grammar sketch;
- cross-linguistic breadth index;
- evidence audit tying every major generalization to examples;
- unresolved-question list and next elicitation plan;
- final mixed morphology/syntax practical;
- oral defense.

### Bridge forward
Read DIU's descriptions of AL4406 Field Methods and AL5313 Advanced Grammatical Analysis and explicitly identify what you are now prepared to do and what remains for supervised/formal study.

# Reading response standard

For substantial chapters/articles record:

1. full citation;
2. analytical question addressed;
3. diagnostic/procedure introduced;
4. evidence used;
5. one worked example reconstructed in your own words;
6. distinction between form, function, and terminology;
7. typological caution;
8. competing analysis if one is plausible;
9. application to an unfamiliar dataset;
10. possible application to Egyptian Arabic;
11. new elicitation question.

For Egyptian Arabic scholarship add:

12. frozen pre-reading analysis;
13. points of agreement/disagreement;
14. whether the publication uses a theoretical framework beyond this course's required level;
15. any revision made to your description and the evidence that justified it.
