# AL4410 Reading Plan

## Primary backbone

### J. Albert Bickford — *Morphology and Syntax: Tools for Analyzing the World's Languages*
Use systematically. The SIL table of contents aligns unusually well with the modeled course:
- preliminaries and terminology;
- morphemes/hypotheses;
- constituent structure, syntactic categories, grammatical relations;
- phrase structure and noun phrases;
- valence and obliques;
- inflection and derivation;
- suppletion/morphophonemics;
- nonlinear affixation;
- constituent order, questions, commands;
- case/agreement, clitics, passive/voice;
- embedded and relative clauses;
- grammar-sketch writing.

Exact pages should be assigned from the edition actually owned.

## Secondary/free reference

### *Essentials of Linguistics*, 2nd ed.
Use morphology and syntax chapters for review, terminology checks, and alternate explanations. It is supplementary, not the analytical depth standard.

## Additional references

Add focused typological or descriptive references as the course reaches topics that need more breadth, especially:
- grammatical relations/alignment;
- valence-changing operations;
- clitics/wordhood;
- tense/aspect/mood;
- relative clauses and complementation.

Prefer primary/descriptive linguistic sources over generic language-learning sites.

## Egyptian Arabic reference rule

Published grammars and academic descriptions are **comparison sources**, not initial answer keys.

For each major Egyptian Arabic phenomenon:
1. collect/organize original data;
2. perform independent analysis;
3. freeze it;
4. consult scholarly descriptions;
5. record agreements, disagreements, and revisions.

## Reading notes

For each substantial reading capture:
- central claim/diagnostic;
- data used;
- analytical method;
- a worked example in your own words;
- typological caution;
- potential application to Egyptian Arabic;
- question requiring more data.
