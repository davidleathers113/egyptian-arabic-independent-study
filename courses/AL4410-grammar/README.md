# AL4410 Independent Study — Principles of Grammatical Analysis

**Modeled DIU course:** AL4410 Principles of Grammatical Analysis  
**Modeled credits:** 4  
**Target workload:** 180 documented hours  
**Recommended sequence:** after AL4302 and AL4303

## Purpose

Develop practical skill in analyzing and describing morphology and syntax in natural languages. The course emphasizes working from data, generating and testing hypotheses, and learning enough typological breadth that English, Biblical languages, and Arabic do not become unconscious templates for how grammar “should” work.

DIU publicly describes AL4410 as training students through numerous practical exercises from a large variety of languages to determine word classes and allomorphy, analyze inflectional and derivational morphology, and analyze phrases, clauses, and sentences.

## Egyptian Arabic role

Egyptian Arabic is the sustained field application. It is especially useful for:
- root-and-pattern/nonconcatenative morphology;
- clitics and affix-like material;
- pronouns and agreement;
- verbal paradigms;
- noun/adjective phrases;
- negation and interrogation;
- clause structure;
- valence and argument structure;
- complex sentences.

But at least **half of formal analysis exercises** should use non-Arabic datasets.

## Major outputs

1. Cross-linguistic morphology problem sets.
2. Cross-linguistic syntax problem sets.
3. Egyptian Arabic paradigm database.
4. Morpheme/allomorph analyses.
5. Root-and-pattern morphology study.
6. Constituent and grammatical-relation analyses.
7. Clause/complex-sentence analyses.
8. Mini grammatical sketch developed throughout the course.
9. AI grammar-induction benchmark.
10. Midterm examinations and final practical/oral defense.
11. **Preliminary Grammatical Sketch of Egyptian Arabic Speech from SPK001**.
