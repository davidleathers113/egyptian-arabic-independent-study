# Syllabus — AL4410 Principles of Grammatical Analysis — Egyptian Arabic

## Course purpose

Develop reliable techniques for analyzing and describing basic morphology and syntax from natural-language data, with heavy practical problem solving and a cumulative Egyptian Arabic grammar project.

## Workload

- 180 documented hours
- 16 instructional weeks
- target average: 11.25 hours/week
- at least 60% active analysis/problem solving/data work

## Core domains

1. Morphemes, morphs, hypotheses, segmentation.
2. Word classes determined from language-specific evidence.
3. Allomorphy and conditioning.
4. Inflectional morphology.
5. Derivational morphology.
6. Suppletion and morphophonemics.
7. Nonlinear/nonconcatenative morphology.
8. Constituent structure and syntactic categories.
9. Grammatical relations and argument structure.
10. Phrase structure and noun phrases.
11. Valence, obliques, and selected voice operations.
12. Constituent-order variation.
13. Negation, questions, and commands.
14. Embedded and relative clauses.
15. Clitics/word-division problems.
16. Writing a cautious grammar sketch.

## Primary working text

**J. Albert Bickford, _Morphology and Syntax: Tools for Analyzing the World's Languages_.** Use as the main analytical backbone. Its structure directly spans morphemes/hypotheses, syntax, inflection/derivation, nonlinear affixation, case/agreement, clitics, voice, embedded/relative clauses, and grammar-sketch writing.

## Supporting resources

- *Essentials of Linguistics*, 2nd ed., morphology/syntax chapters for accessible review.
- Additional typological/grammatical references chosen per topic.
- Published Egyptian Arabic grammars/descriptions used only after independent analysis is frozen for the relevant phenomenon.

## Cross-linguistic requirement

At least half of graded analytical datasets must come from languages outside Arabic. Include languages with substantially different morphological and syntactic profiles where possible.

## Assessment model

- cross-linguistic morphology/syntax problem sets: 30%
- Egyptian Arabic labs/paradigms: 15%
- morphology midterm: 10%
- syntax midterm: 10%
- root-and-pattern case study: 5%
- clause/complex-sentence project: 5%
- AI grammar-induction benchmark: 5%
- final grammar sketch: 15%
- final practical/oral defense: 5%

## Mastery standard

85% overall plus demonstrated competence in both morphology and syntax. A student who excels in Arabic morphology but cannot analyze unfamiliar syntax has not mastered the course.

## Academic integrity

Human analysis precedes AI or answer-key consultation for graded data. See `/ACADEMIC-INTEGRITY.md`.
