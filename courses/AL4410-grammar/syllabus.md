# Syllabus — AL4410 Principles of Grammatical Analysis — Egyptian Arabic

## Course purpose

Develop reliable techniques for analyzing and describing **basic morphology and syntax from natural-language data**, with heavy practical problem solving, explicit cross-linguistic transfer, disciplined interlinear glossing, and a cumulative Egyptian Arabic grammar project.

The course is modeled on DIU's public AL4410 scope rather than on a generic university syntax survey. DIU emphasizes numerous practical exercises from many languages, determining word classes and allomorphy, handling inflectional and derivational morphology, and analyzing phrases, clauses, and sentences.

## Workload

- 180 documented hours
- 16 instructional weeks
- target average: 11.25 hours/week
- at least 60% active analysis/problem solving/data work

## Core domains

1. Morphemes, morphs, hypotheses, segmentation, and evidence.
2. Word classes determined from language-specific diagnostics rather than English labels.
3. Allomorphy, suppletion, and conditioning.
4. Inflectional morphology.
5. Derivational morphology and valence-changing morphology.
6. Nonlinear/nonconcatenative morphology.
7. Wordhood, clitics, and word-division problems.
8. Constituent structure and syntactic categories.
9. Grammatical relations, semantic roles, argument structure, case, and agreement.
10. Phrase structure and noun phrases.
11. Valence, obliques, passive/voice, and related operations.
12. Constituent-order variation and typological comparison.
13. Negation, questions, and commands.
14. Embedded, adverbial, complement, and relative clauses.
15. Interlinear glossing and presentation of grammatical evidence.
16. Writing a cautious grammar sketch grounded in examples.

## Primary working text

**J. Albert Bickford, _Morphology and Syntax: Tools for Analyzing the World's Languages_ (SIL International; 2022 casebound reissue of the established text).**

Use as the main analytical backbone. Its 27 chapters directly cover morphemes/hypotheses, constituent structure, syntactic categories, grammatical relations, phrase structure, noun phrases, valence, obliques, inflection, derivation, suppletion/morphophonemics, nonlinear affixation, variable constituent order, questions, commands, case/agreement, clitics, voice, embedded clauses, relative clauses, linguistic writing, and grammar-sketch preparation.

## Major secondary text

**Thomas E. Payne, _Exploring Language Structure: A Student's Guide_.**

Use as the principal second explanation and additional problem source. Cambridge describes it as a step-by-step guide to analyzing the morphology and syntax of any language with almost 100 practical exercises drawn from diverse languages. Its integrated chapters on morphological processes, morphophonemics, word classes, constituent structure, typology, grammatical relations, voice/valence, and multi-clause constructions make it unusually well matched to AL4410.

## Supporting resources

- _Essentials of Linguistics_, 2nd ed., morphology and syntax chapters for free review/self-checking.
- Rochelle Lieber, _Introducing Morphology_, 3rd ed., selectively for modern morphology terminology, inflection, typology, and morphology-syntax/phonology interfaces.
- WALS Online and Grambank for **post-analysis typological comparison**, not as substitutes for primary analysis.
- Leipzig Glossing Rules for a consistent baseline interlinear-glossing convention.
- MIT OCW 24.900 for introductory morphology/syntax practice and a native-speaker syntax-data assignment.
- MIT OCW 24.902 for optional theory-heavier syntax stretch work after descriptive competence is established.
- SIL FieldWorks Language Explorer (FLEx) as an optional corpus/interlinearization tool **after manual segmentation and analysis are understood**.
- Published Egyptian Arabic grammars/descriptions used only after independent analysis is frozen for the relevant phenomenon.

## Egyptian Arabic role

Egyptian Arabic is the longitudinal application language, not the source of the course's generalizations. For every substantial grammatical claim distinguish:

- raw utterance/token;
- segmentation;
- morpheme gloss;
- lexical category hypothesis;
- grammatical feature/function;
- syntactic constituency/relationship claim;
- counterexamples;
- consultant judgment or corpus evidence;
- confidence level;
- alternative analysis.

## Cross-linguistic requirement

At least half of graded analytical datasets must come from languages outside Arabic. The final breadth portfolio must include materially different grammatical profiles, including where available:

- isolating and highly synthetic morphology;
- concatenative and nonconcatenative morphology;
- head- and dependent-marking patterns;
- case-rich and case-poor systems;
- agreement systems unlike English/Arabic;
- different dominant constituent orders;
- a language whose grammatical-relation diagnostics resist simple English subject/object assumptions;
- at least one substantially unfamiliar relative/complement-clause strategy.

## Assessment model

- cross-linguistic morphology/syntax problem sets: 30%
- Egyptian Arabic labs/paradigms/interlinear examples: 15%
- morphology midterm: 10%
- syntax midterm: 10%
- root-and-pattern/nonlinear morphology case study: 5%
- complex-clause project: 5%
- AI/morphological-analyzer benchmark: 5%
- final grammar sketch: 15%
- final practical/oral defense: 5%

## Mastery standard

85% overall **plus** demonstrated competence in both morphology and syntax. A student who excels in Egyptian Arabic morphology but cannot segment unfamiliar data, diagnose word classes, reason about grammatical relations, or analyze unfamiliar clause structure has not mastered the course.

## Theory boundary

The course builds enough theoretical literacy to describe evidence clearly, but it does not attempt to reproduce DIU's later **AL5313 Advanced Grammatical Analysis**, which explicitly incorporates syntactic typology, formal syntax, and a simplified Lexical Functional Grammar framework. Likewise, discourse-level participant reference, macrosegmentation, information structure, and paragraph analysis belong primarily to AL5312 Discourse Analysis.

## Academic integrity

Human analysis precedes AI, morphological analyzers, answer keys, reference grammars, WALS/Grambank lookup, or published analyses for graded data. See `/ACADEMIC-INTEGRITY.md`.
