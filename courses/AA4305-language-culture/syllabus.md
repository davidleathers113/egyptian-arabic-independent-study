# Syllabus — AA4305 Second Language and Culture Acquisition — Egyptian Arabic

## Course purpose

Develop the ability to **direct, evaluate, and continually improve one's own second-language and culture acquisition** through meaningful participation, native-speaker relationships, strategic practice, reflection, and evidence-based adaptation.

This independent study is modeled on Dallas International University's public AA4305 scope: learner-directed second language and culture acquisition, emphasis on beginning-stage processes, examination of perspectives on language/culture learning, strategies for dealing appropriately with cultural differences, and practical work with a native speaker in a small-group/non-instructed lab setting.

The course is not primarily an Arabic class and is not a survey of SLA theory. Egyptian Arabic is the sustained application language through which the learner demonstrates that the acquisition system works in practice.

## Workload

- **135 documented hours**
- **12 instructional weeks**
- target average: **11.25 hours/week**
- minimum **24 hours of documented acquisition-lab/native-speaker participation**
- substantial additional Egyptian Arabic listening/speaking and lifestyle participation every week

The 24-hour practicum requirement is an independent-study design standard, not a claim about DIU's unpublished hour requirement.

## Course boundary

### This course must build

1. learner-directed acquisition planning;
2. goal setting and needs analysis;
3. comprehension and negotiated interaction;
4. meaning-focused listening and speaking;
5. deliberate vocabulary/multiword-unit development;
6. strategic attention to pronunciation and form;
7. fluency development;
8. communication repair and willingness to communicate;
9. feedback/correction strategy;
10. culture learning through participation and observation;
11. reflective self-regulation and strategy revision;
12. ethical, relationship-centered work with native speakers;
13. ability to distinguish language-learning resources from evidence about actual host-language use;
14. a sustainable continuation plan beyond the course.

### This course does not try to become

- a complete survey of SLA theory;
- an official ACTFL proficiency test;
- a traditional grammar course;
- another phonetics/phonology/grammar-analysis course;
- a complete ethnography of Egyptian society;
- an Arabic culture fact-memorization course;
- a full Growing Participator Approach phase certification;
- a substitute for real interaction through AI simulation.

AL4302/AL4303/AL4410 knowledge is used diagnostically when helpful, but communicative participation remains primary.

## Curriculum architecture

### 1. Academic SLA backbone

**Patsy M. Lightbown & Nina Spada, _How Languages Are Learned_, 5th ed. (2021).**

Use selected chapters as the main academic introduction to second-language acquisition research, individual differences, learning conditions, interaction, feedback, instruction, and common claims about language learning.

### 2. Practicum backbone

**Growing Participator Approach (GPA) official materials.**

Use GPA as a major field-learning influence: growing through participation in relationships and host life, comprehension-rich nurturer sessions, recordings that support later growth, and movement from specially designed participation toward increasingly natural lifestyle participation.

Do **not** claim to complete a GPA phase solely because this 135-hour course borrows its activities. GPA's own phase model assumes substantially larger amounts of special-growth participation than this course guarantees.

### 3. Course-balance framework

**Paul Nation's Four Strands.**

Audit the overall learning program for reasonable balance among:

- meaning-focused input;
- meaning-focused output;
- language-focused learning;
- fluency development.

The strands are a planning diagnostic, not a rigid stopwatch rule for every individual week.

### 4. Vocabulary reference

**I. S. P. Nation, _Learning Vocabulary in Another Language_, 3rd ed. (2022).**

Use selectively for what it means to know a word, vocabulary in listening/speaking, learning outside class, learner autonomy, context, deliberate word-card learning, multiword units, and course design.

### 5. Culture-learning method

**James P. Spradley, _Participant Observation_.**

Use selected steps from the Developmental Research Sequence to make culture learning systematic: keep an ethnographic record, make descriptive observations, develop cultural domains as hypotheses, focus observations, look for contrasts, and revise cultural interpretations before generalizing.

This is adapted for respectful learner participation, not used to turn a friendship or coaching relationship into covert research.

### 6. Functional progress framework

**ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines 2024** may be used descriptively through the FACT dimensions:

- Functions and tasks;
- Accuracy;
- Context and content;
- Text type.

Self-evaluation in this repo is **not an official ACTFL proficiency rating**.

## Egyptian Arabic practical-resource rule

Egyptian Arabic coursebooks, audio, podcasts, videos, dictionaries, songs, and other materials support practice but do not define the academic curriculum.

The **Kallimni ‘Arabi** series from AUC Press is a preferred optional structured-resource family because it focuses on spoken Egyptian Arabic, listening/speaking, native-speaker audio, real-life situations, and functional communication. Select the volume that matches the learner's actual starting level rather than restarting artificially at Book 1.

The learner's native-speaker consultant remains a source of **speaker-specific evidence**, not an automatic representative of every Egyptian speaker or all Egyptian culture.

## Four-Strands participation audit

Across the course, track approximate time in:

| Strand | Examples in this study |
|---|---|
| Meaning-focused input | coach stories, comprehensible interaction, graded/native audio, listening for meaning |
| Meaning-focused output | conversations, retells, role-play, explanations, real-world tasks |
| Language-focused learning | targeted pronunciation, vocabulary review, form noticing, selective grammar clarification |
| Fluency development | repeated familiar stories/tasks, faster retrieval, repeated listening, familiar-topic conversation |

A large persistent imbalance should trigger a strategy revision.

## Practicum

Minimum **24 documented hours** of learner-directed acquisition-lab interaction with native speaker(s), ideally about 2 hours/week, separate from ordinary tutoring where possible.

The practicum should progress according to **current ability**, not calendar week alone:

- highly supported comprehension and shared context;
- increasing interaction/repair;
- descriptions and stories;
- retelling and information-gap work;
- increasingly spontaneous conversation;
- practical/lifestyle tasks;
- culturally meaningful participation.

If the learner already exceeds an early-stage activity, move forward rather than performing it merely to satisfy a syllabus.

## Feedback policy

Do not ask the language partner to correct every error in real time.

For each lab, preselect the feedback mode:

- communication first / minimal correction;
- recasts/confirmation only;
- one or two target features;
- delayed debrief;
- explicit correction when communication meaningfully breaks down.

The learner should study which feedback arrangements improve later performance rather than assuming more correction is always better.

## Culture-learning policy

Culture evidence is recorded using four labels:

1. **observation** — what happened;
2. **interpretation** — what it might mean;
3. **host perspective** — what one or more people said about it;
4. **confidence/generalizability** — how far the learner can responsibly extend the claim.

One person's statement is evidence about that person's perspective. It does not by itself establish a rule for Egyptians, Arabs, Muslims, Christians, a generation, a region, or a social class.

## Assessment model

- acquisition-lab participation/evidence: **25%**
- learning journal and strategy experiments: **15%**
- listening/speaking functional portfolio: **15%**
- culture-learning/participant-observation portfolio: **15%**
- reading/theory-to-practice responses: **10%**
- mid-course strategy and Four-Strands audit: **5%**
- AI language-coach experiment: **5%**
- final portfolio + oral defense/continuation plan: **10%**

## Mastery standard

Target **85% overall** plus demonstrated ability to:

- plan learner-directed acquisition;
- gather evidence about what is/isn't working;
- revise strategies;
- participate in unrehearsed Egyptian Arabic beyond the starting baseline;
- manage communication breakdown;
- learn culture without premature stereotyping;
- build a sustainable independent-learning system.

No particular unofficial proficiency label is required. Growth is judged against comparable baseline/final tasks and functional evidence.

## Academic integrity and AI

AI may support planning, practice, feedback, transcript comparison, experiment design, and additional low-stakes interaction.

AI may not:

- replace the native-speaker practicum;
- fabricate participation evidence;
- become the authoritative judge of Egyptian Arabic naturalness or culture;
- generate reflective journals presented as lived experience;
- convert rehearsed/generated language into evidence of spontaneous ability.

See `/ACADEMIC-INTEGRITY.md`.
