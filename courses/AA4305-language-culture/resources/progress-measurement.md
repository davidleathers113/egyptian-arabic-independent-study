# Functional Progress Measurement Framework

## Purpose

Measure growth in Egyptian Arabic without pretending that a self-directed independent study can issue an official proficiency rating.

The framework borrows the **FACT** dimensions from ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines 2024 while adding interaction/repair, comprehension, fluency, willingness-to-communicate, and learner-autonomy evidence that matter to AA4305.

## Rule

Use the same or closely comparable **unrehearsed** tasks at baseline, mid-course, and final.

Do not memorize a script and then call improved delivery language acquisition.

---

# Core task families

Choose 4–6 that match actual goals.

## A. Social interaction
- greet/open conversation;
- exchange personal information;
- ask follow-up questions;
- close interaction naturally.

## B. Description
- describe a picture/place/person/event;
- explain what is happening;
- compare two options.

## C. Narration
- recount something that happened;
- retell a short story;
- sequence events.

## D. Practical problem solving
- explain a problem;
- ask for information/help;
- arrange plans;
- negotiate a change/misunderstanding.

## E. Opinion/reasoning
- state preference;
- give reasons;
- compare alternatives;
- respond to a follow-up challenge.

## F. Listening
- understand a short natural story/explanation;
- identify gist;
- answer content questions;
- summarize/retell;
- ask clarification where needed.

# Functional rubric

Score 1–5 for internal comparison only. These scores are **course-created**, not ACTFL levels.

## 1. Functions/tasks

**1** — cannot reliably perform task without English/modeling.  
**2** — completes fragments of task with extensive partner support.  
**3** — completes basic task with pauses/repair and some support.  
**4** — completes task independently and handles common follow-up.  
**5** — expands/adapts task spontaneously when conversation changes.

## 2. Comprehensibility / accuracy that matters

**1** — frequent breakdown prevents core message.  
**2** — partner must repeatedly infer/repair meaning.  
**3** — message generally understandable despite recurring errors.  
**4** — errors rarely disrupt communication; learner can self-repair.  
**5** — sustained clear communication with appropriate control for task.

Do not grade accent against native-speaker imitation unless a specific phonetic contrast affects communication.

## 3. Context/content range

**1** — isolated memorized topics only.  
**2** — narrow familiar contexts.  
**3** — multiple common personal/everyday contexts.  
**4** — broader familiar social/practical topics and some unexpected turns.  
**5** — adapts to a substantial range of relevant contexts/topics.

## 4. Text type / connectedness

**1** — isolated words/fixed chunks.  
**2** — short phrases/basic sentence fragments.  
**3** — strings of sentences / simple connected speech.  
**4** — sustained connected description/narration/explanation.  
**5** — organized extended discourse appropriate to task.

## 5. Interaction and repair

**1** — breakdown often ends interaction/requires English rescue.  
**2** — uses a small number of prompted repair moves.  
**3** — independently asks repetition/clarification and confirms meaning.  
**4** — flexibly rephrases, checks understanding, and keeps interaction moving.  
**5** — manages complex breakdown/follow-up naturally with little external rescue.

## 6. Listening/comprehension

**1** — recognizes isolated familiar items only.  
**2** — follows highly supported short speech.  
**3** — follows gist and selected details in familiar natural speech.  
**4** — follows connected speech with strategic clarification/replay.  
**5** — follows sustained relevant speech and infers/repairs gaps effectively.

## 7. Fluency on familiar material

Assess only tasks containing mostly known language/content.

**1** — severe retrieval difficulty; message frequently stalls.  
**2** — frequent long pauses/restarts.  
**3** — workable flow with noticeable searching.  
**4** — generally smooth retrieval with occasional hesitation.  
**5** — sustained efficient production appropriate to current task complexity.

Fluency is not simply fast speech.

# Willingness-to-communicate evidence

WTC is context-sensitive, so do not reduce it to one personality score.

Weekly log 3 situations:

| Situation | Opportunity existed? | Did I initiate/respond? | Anxiety/avoidance 1–5 | What helped/hurt? | Next action |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|

Track behavior such as:
- starting conversations;
- asking follow-up questions;
- continuing despite uncertainty;
- choosing Arabic instead of switching immediately to English;
- participating with speakers beyond the primary coach where appropriate.

Growth means more effective participation, not absence of nervousness.

---

# Learner-autonomy evidence

At baseline/mid/final, score ability to:

1. identify a real communicative need;
2. design a useful native-speaker activity;
3. obtain appropriate input/examples;
4. choose a feedback mode;
5. diagnose a breakdown;
6. select a strategy;
7. test whether it works;
8. redesign the next session;
9. find independent/lifestyle participation opportunities;
10. distinguish when formal teaching is actually needed.

Use evidence from actual sessions rather than self-description only.

# Vocabulary evidence

Do not count `number of flashcards` as primary progress.

Sample 20–30 personally relevant items/chunks and test:

- recognition in natural speech;
- meaning in context;
- productive retrieval;
- appropriate frame/collocation;
- naturalness/register confirmed where needed;
- delayed retention;
- spontaneous occurrence in actual interaction.

---

# Culture-learning evidence

Score culture case studies qualitatively rather than testing trivia.

Evidence of growth includes:
- more concrete observation;
- fewer premature generalizations;
- ability to identify one's own expectations;
- seeking host perspective respectfully;
- noticing contextual variation;
- revising interpretations;
- improved pragmatic participation.

See `culture-learning-fieldwork.md`.

# Progress claim standard

A final claim should look like:

> At baseline I could describe familiar personal information but could not sustain an unrehearsed past-event narrative without extensive English help. In the final comparable task I produced X minutes of connected speech, independently repaired Y breakdowns, answered follow-up questions, and required English rescue only Z times. See artifacts A/B.

Not:

> I became intermediate.

If an external official assessment is later taken, store that result separately from this internal framework.
