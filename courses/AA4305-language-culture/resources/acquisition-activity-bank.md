# Learner-Directed Acquisition Activity Bank

## Purpose

Provide reusable activities the learner can select/adapt with a native Egyptian Arabic speaker without turning the lab into conventional teacher-led lessons.

These are original/adaptable task patterns influenced by communicative language learning, Growing Participator Approach principles, Nation's Four Strands, and field-language-learning practice. They are not reproductions of copyrighted GPA or coursebook lessons.

## Tags

- `MFI` — meaning-focused input
- `MFO` — meaning-focused output
- `LFL` — language-focused learning
- `FLU` — fluency development
- `CUL` — culture-learning opportunity
- `REP` — communication repair

Choose tasks based on actual ability.

---

# 1. Quick-Do Expansion

**Tags:** MFI, MFO, FLU

## Goal
Understand/action familiar verbs and spatial language rapidly in changing combinations.

## Materials
Objects in room or simple pictures.

## Procedure
1. Partner gives short natural instructions.
2. Learner acts without translating aloud.
3. Add combinations/sequences.
4. Learner eventually gives instructions back.
5. End with natural questions about what happened.

## Level up
- longer sequences;
- faster natural delivery;
- less visible context;
- learner narrates actions afterward.

## Evidence
Comprehension latency, need for repetition, output fluency.

---

# 2. Picture Choice → Explanation

**Tags:** MFI, MFO, REP

## Goal
Move from recognition to explanation/reasoning.

## Procedure
1. Lay out 4–8 images.
2. Partner describes one without naming it.
3. Learner selects.
4. Reverse roles.
5. Add `why`, comparison, or prediction questions.

## Level up
Use complex scenes, similar images, or culturally meaningful situations.

---

# 3. Find the Difference

**Tags:** MFI, MFO, REP

## Goal
Sustain information-gap interaction.

Each participant has a similar but non-identical picture/list/map.

No showing materials initially. Ask/answer until differences are found.

## Evidence
Question formation, repair moves, descriptive vocabulary, persistence.

---

# 4. Known-World Story

**Tags:** MFI, CUL

## Goal
Understand longer speech by anchoring it in shared knowledge.

Partner tells a story about:
- something that happened during the week;
- a mutual acquaintance/event;
- a familiar place;
- a known routine.

Learner listens first for meaning, asks clarification, then summarizes.

## Level up
Ask motivations, causes, consequences, opinions, alternate outcomes.

---

# 5. Wordless Picture Story

**Tags:** MFI, MFO, FLU

## Goal
Build narrative comprehension/production without written-language dependence.

1. Partner tells story from wordless sequence.
2. Learner identifies sequence/meaning.
3. Replay/tell again.
4. Learner retells.
5. Retell again days later.

## Evidence
Connectedness, recurring chunks, tense/aspect access, fluency.

---

# 6. Same Story, Three Times

**Tags:** MFO, FLU

## Goal
Develop fluency rather than new linguistic knowledge.

Tell a familiar personal story:
- attempt 1 naturally;
- receive only essential feedback;
- attempt 2 later with same partner;
- attempt 3 another day or context.

Track pauses, English rescue, connectedness, repair, elaboration.

Do not memorize a script.

---

# 7. Explain It Without the Word

**Tags:** MFO, REP, FLU

## Goal
Develop circumlocution/communication resilience.

Learner explains a familiar object/concept without using its name. Partner guesses.

Reverse roles.

Useful for practicing what to do when vocabulary disappears in real conversation.

---

# 8. Repair Challenge

**Tags:** REP, MFO, MFI

## Goal
Automate clarification/repair moves.

Partner deliberately includes occasional:
- fast phrase;
- unknown word;
- ambiguous reference;
- incomplete information.

Learner must repair in Egyptian Arabic instead of switching immediately to English.

Keep artificial breakdowns limited so interaction remains natural.

---

# 9. Route / Map Task

**Tags:** MFI, MFO, REP

## Goal
Practice directions, location, sequencing, confirmation.

Use a map of a real or imagined neighborhood.

Tasks:
- explain route;
- ask follow-up;
- intentionally misunderstand one step;
- choose between routes and explain preference.

---

# 10. Plan Something Together

**Tags:** MFO, REP, CUL

## Goal
Negotiate a real decision.

Examples:
- meal;
- outing;
- schedule;
- shopping list;
- trip idea;
- family activity.

Must include conflicting preferences/constraints.

Culture-learning opportunity: observe how suggestions, refusals, disagreement, and compromise are phrased.

---

# 11. Personal Photo Conversation

**Tags:** MFI, MFO, CUL

## Goal
Connect language to real people/events and deeper relational knowledge.

With appropriate privacy, learner or partner uses a few non-sensitive personal photos to prompt stories/questions.

Do not record/publish private stories without permission.

---

# 12. Object / Practice Ethnography

**Tags:** CUL, MFI

## Goal
Learn cultural categories through concrete things/practices.

Choose something ordinary:
- food item;
- household object;
- clothing item;
- social-media convention;
- celebration item;
- phrase used in a recurring situation.

Ask:
- What is it called?
- Who uses it?
- When/where?
- What variations exist?
- What would be unusual?

Record observations as hypotheses, not facts about all Egyptians.

---

# 13. Scenario Naturalness Test

**Tags:** MFO, CUL, LFL

## Goal
Learn pragmatic/register choices.

Present a realistic situation and 2–3 possible phrases you might use.

Ask partner:
- which sounds most natural?;
- what relationship changes the answer?;
- what would sound too formal/direct/strange?;
- what would they actually say?

Learner then role-plays using the preferred options.

---

# 14. Cultural Contrast Interview — Narrow Form

**Tags:** CUL, MFI

## Goal
Explore variation without forcing national stereotypes.

Instead of asking:
> “What do Egyptians do when...?”

Ask:
> “In your family/friend group, what usually happens when...?”

Then:
> “Do you know people who do this differently?”

Use to discover contextual variation.

---

# 15. Native-Speed Ladder

**Tags:** MFI, FLU

## Goal
Increase tolerance of natural speech without turning everything into slow teacher speech.

Use one short recording:
1. natural speed;
2. supported replay/context;
3. focused replay of difficult portion;
4. natural speed again;
5. new similar material at natural speed.

The goal is better processing, not memorizing one clip.

---

# 16. Chunk Harvest → Reuse

**Tags:** LFL, MFO, FLU

## Goal
Turn recurring multiword units into accessible spontaneous language.

1. Notice 3–5 useful chunks from real interaction.
2. Confirm meaning/register.
3. Store with original context/audio if permitted.
4. Generate 2–3 new situations where each chunk fits.
5. Use naturally over the next week.
6. Record whether it appeared spontaneously.

---

# 17. Focused Feedback Replay

**Tags:** LFL, MFO, FLU

## Goal
Test whether correction actually changes performance.

1. Perform a short communicative task.
2. Partner gives feedback on **one target category**.
3. Practice a few examples.
4. Repeat similar task immediately.
5. Test again several days later.

Delayed transfer matters more than immediate imitation.

---

# 18. Mini-Immersion Block

**Tags:** MFI, MFO, REP, FLU

## Goal
Sustain Egyptian Arabic without turning difficulty into failure.

Set 15–30 minutes:
- Egyptian Arabic default;
- repair strategies required;
- English allowed only under defined rescue conditions.

Afterward analyze why switches happened.

Gradually lengthen when useful.

---

# 19. Real-World Voice Message Exchange

**Tags:** MFI, MFO, FLU

## Goal
Practice asynchronous everyday speech.

Exchange short voice messages with consent:
- update;
- question;
- story;
- plan;
- reaction.

Avoid endless rerecording. Set a limit (for example max two attempts) so the sample remains close to real production.

---

# 20. Functional Can-Do Challenge

**Tags:** MFO, REP, CUL

## Goal
Test one real communicative function from the course task inventory.

Examples:
- explain why plans changed;
- politely decline and propose an alternative;
- tell an amusing incident;
- ask someone to explain an unfamiliar cultural reference;
- resolve a small misunderstanding.

Use the internal functional rubric afterward.

---

# Activity-design template

Before creating a new task, fill:

**Need:** What real-world ability am I building?  
**Primary strand:** MFI / MFO / LFL / FLU  
**Current ability:** What can I already do?  
**New challenge:** What becomes harder?  
**Context support:** What makes meaning accessible?  
**Partner role:** What do I need from the native speaker?  
**Feedback mode:** A / B / C / D / E  
**Repair requirement:** What happens if I do not understand?  
**Evidence:** How will I know whether this helped?  
**Level-up rule:** What makes it harder next time?  
**Culture/privacy note:** Any sensitive issue?

A good learner-directed activity makes the language partner's role easy to understand without requiring them to invent a lesson.
