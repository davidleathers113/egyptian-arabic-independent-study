# Acquisition Lab Protocol — Egyptian Arabic

## Purpose

Create a repeatable learner-directed native-speaker lab analogous in spirit to DIU's practical non-instructed component while respecting that the existing coach may also provide ordinary tutoring outside the lab.

## Roles

### Learner
The learner designs the activity, communicates goals simply, tolerates ambiguity, notices breakdowns, and decides what to revisit.

### Nurturer / language partner
The native speaker supplies natural language, interaction, demonstrations, judgments, stories, and cultural perspective. The partner does not need linguistics or teaching expertise.

## Separate lab mode from tutoring mode

At the beginning of a session explicitly mark the lab segment. During lab mode, avoid defaulting to:
- long English grammar explanations;
- word-by-word translation of everything;
- scripted textbook teaching;
- correcting every error immediately.

Instead prefer:
- pointing/actions/objects;
- pictures and scenes;
- choices and contrasts;
- demonstrations;
- short stories;
- repetition with meaning preserved;
- clarification in Arabic where feasible;
- natural recasts and confirmation.

## Standard 60-minute lab

### 1. Warm comprehension — 10 min
Known material in new combinations; low pressure.

### 2. New participation activity — 20 min
Introduce one manageable communicative domain using rich context.

### 3. Interaction expansion — 15 min
Questions, role changes, descriptions, choices, retell, or information gap.

### 4. Connected speech — 10 min
Short story, explanation, or conversation above the learner's comfortable level but supported by context.

### 5. Debrief — 5 min
After the lab mode, briefly clarify the most important unresolved issues. Do not turn the debrief into a complete translation of everything that occurred.

## Activity progression

Early:
- Quick-Do/action commands;
- object identification/manipulation;
- picture descriptions;
- who/what/where contrasts;
- simple routines.

Middle:
- scene descriptions;
- picture sequences;
- personal stories;
- guessing/information-gap tasks;
- event retells;
- practical role-play.

Later:
- unscripted conversation;
- explanations/opinions;
- narratives;
- problem-solving/planning;
- culturally meaningful topics and interactions.

## Session record

Log:
`date | minutes | activity | % Arabic estimate | comprehension successes | breakdowns | repair strategies | new recurring forms | culture observations | next-session change`

The `% Arabic` field is a rough reflection, not a scientific measurement.

## Recording

Record only with explicit permission. Participation evidence can also be documented through notes if a session should remain unrecorded.

## Culture rule

A statement by one Egyptian speaker is one perspective, not automatically “Egyptian culture.” Record observations as hypotheses and seek multiple examples/perspectives before generalizing.
