# Acquisition Lab Protocol — Egyptian Arabic

## Purpose

Create a repeatable **learner-directed native-speaker practicum** analogous in spirit to DIU AA4305's public lab description: a small-group/non-instructed setting in which the learner develops the ability to generate and manage acquisition opportunities rather than depending entirely on formal instruction.

This protocol is strongly influenced by the Growing Participator Approach (GPA) but is not represented as an official GPA course or phase completion.

## Minimum course requirement

- **24 documented lab hours** across the 12-week course.
- Aim for roughly **2 hours/week** of explicit acquisition-lab mode.
- Additional ordinary tutoring, friendship, conversation, and lifestyle participation are strongly encouraged and logged separately.

The 24-hour floor is an independent-study design choice, not a published DIU requirement.

---

# Roles

## Learner / growing participator

The learner:

- identifies current communicative needs;
- selects/designs activities;
- communicates the activity to the partner simply;
- tolerates incomplete understanding;
- asks for repetition/rephrasing/help strategically;
- notices recurring language without stopping every interaction;
- decides what to revisit;
- keeps records;
- evaluates which techniques work;
- gradually takes more responsibility for finding interaction beyond the lab.

## Nurturer / language partner

The native speaker supplies:

- natural language;
- shared context;
- demonstrations/actions;
- pictures/stories/experiences;
- interaction;
- clarification;
- judgments when genuinely needed;
- their own cultural perspective;
- natural recasts or agreed correction.

The partner does **not** need linguistics terminology or professional teaching expertise.

## Coach/teacher mode vs. lab mode

The same person may serve in both roles, but mark the transition explicitly.

### Teacher mode may include
- grammar explanations;
- translations;
- planned instruction;
- explicit drills;
- broad correction;
- textbook work.

### Acquisition-lab mode prioritizes
- meaning;
- interaction;
- contextualized language;
- learner direction;
- communication repair;
- discovery;
- participation;
- selective/delayed feedback.

Both can be useful. The course assesses whether the learner can operate **without requiring teacher mode for every learning event**.

---

# Placement rule

Do not force absolute-beginner activities simply because GPA begins there.

Before each new activity ask:

1. Can I already perform this function comfortably and spontaneously?
2. Is the language/input mostly comprehensible but still growth-producing?
3. Does this activity create something I cannot reliably do yet?
4. Can the difficulty be raised through richer content, faster speech, less visual support, more turns, or more spontaneous follow-up?

If an activity is trivial, increase complexity or move forward.

# Standard 60-minute lab template

## 1. Familiar re-entry / fluency — 10 minutes

Use known material for relatively easy meaningful interaction:
- revisit a familiar story/topic;
- quick retell;
- familiar picture sequence;
- known real-world situation.

Goal: fluent access, confidence, relational reconnection.

## 2. New comprehensible participation — 15 minutes

Introduce one manageable new domain using rich context:
- objects;
- actions;
- pictures;
- maps;
- known people/events;
- demonstrations;
- shared experience.

Goal: meaning-focused input.

## 3. Interaction expansion — 15 minutes

Require the learner to do more:
- choose/contrast;
- ask follow-up questions;
- describe;
- predict;
- solve an information gap;
- explain preferences/reasons;
- take a different role.

Goal: meaning-focused output + repair.

## 4. Connected speech — 10 minutes

Partner provides or co-constructs something longer:
- story;
- explanation;
- personal anecdote;
- description of a cultural practice;
- discussion of something both know.

Learner responds with comprehension, questions, or retell appropriate to level.

## 5. Targeted language focus / feedback — 5 minutes

Only if useful:
- clarify one recurring phrase/construction;
- review one pronunciation/perception issue;
- compare two forms;
- explain a communication failure.

Do not convert this into a full grammar lesson unless leaving lab mode intentionally.

## 6. Debrief / next-step — 5 minutes

Record:
- what worked;
- what repeatedly broke down;
- what was surprisingly easy;
- 1–3 items/questions worth revisiting;
- what should change next session.

# Activity progression bank

Choose by ability, not week number.

## Highly supported comprehension
- Quick-Do/action commands;
- object identification/manipulation;
- picture pointing/selection;
- who/what/where choices;
- simple scene description;
- map/location work;
- known routines.

## Supported interaction
- picture differences;
- information gaps;
- categorize/rank items;
- make choices and give reasons;
- plan a simple event;
- identify mistakes in a scene;
- describe a known person/place.

## Narrative growth
- picture sequences;
- wordless stories;
- coach personal stories;
- learner retells;
- shared-event retells;
- tell the same story on different days;
- compare two versions of an event.

## Real-world function
- arrange plans;
- ask for/help with information;
- explain a problem;
- make/decline requests;
- negotiate preferences;
- give directions;
- recount what happened;
- talk through a purchase/service situation;
- explain an opinion with reasons.

## Rich participation
- unscripted conversation;
- life-story segments;
- opinions/explanations;
- problem-solving;
- discussing media/events both participants know;
- culturally meaningful topics;
- extended narratives and follow-up questions.

# Four-Strands check

At the end of each lab tag approximate portions as:

- `MFI` — meaning-focused input;
- `MFO` — meaning-focused output;
- `LFL` — language-focused learning;
- `FLU` — fluency development.

This is approximate. Do not double-count clock time in the formal hours log.

Review across several sessions for persistent imbalance.

---

# Communication-repair protocol

Build a small high-frequency set of Egyptian Arabic repair tools until they become automatic.

Functional categories:

- repeat;
- slower;
- rephrase/say another way;
- what does X mean?;
- did you mean X?;
- I understood X but not Y;
- example please;
- confirm/reject interpretation;
- stall briefly while processing;
- recover topic after breakdown.

Assess whether the learner **uses these spontaneously**, not merely whether they can translate them.

---

# Corrective-feedback modes

Agree on one mode before the session or activity.

## Mode A — communication first
Correct only errors that block/seriously distort meaning.

## Mode B — natural recast
Partner responds naturally with a corrected/reformulated version but conversation continues.

## Mode C — target-feature focus
Partner flags only 1–2 preselected issues.

## Mode D — delayed feedback
Partner notes important recurring issues and reviews them after the activity.

## Mode E — explicit teaching
Leave acquisition-lab mode intentionally and explain the issue.

The learner should experiment and determine which modes lead to later improvement for which kinds of problem.

---

# Recording protocol

Record only with explicit permission.

Useful recording types:
- short story/input for later replay;
- baseline/final task;
- selected interaction for repair/fluency analysis;
- learner retell;
- pronunciation target where relevant.

Not every conversation needs recording. Relationship quality and privacy matter more than data volume.

Private raw audio follows `/PRIVACY-AND-DATA.md`.

# Session record

Log:

`date | minutes | lab/task | MFI | MFO | LFL | FLU | estimated % Arabic | comprehension successes | breakdowns | repair used | feedback mode | new recurring chunks/forms | culture observations | WTC/avoidance note | next-session change`

`% Arabic` and strand allocations are rough planning reflections, not scientific measurements.

# Culture rule

A statement by one Egyptian speaker is one perspective, not automatically “Egyptian culture.”

When culturally interesting behavior/language appears:

1. record the concrete event;
2. separate observation from interpretation;
3. ask a respectful open-ended question if appropriate;
4. record the person's perspective;
5. seek other examples/perspectives over time;
6. note region/generation/social context only when relevant and safe;
7. revise hypotheses rather than defending them.

Never pressure the language partner to become a spokesperson for an entire nation, religion, class, gender, or generation.

---

# Lifestyle participation

At least weekly, identify one opportunity outside the structured lab to use or encounter Egyptian Arabic naturally when feasible:

- conversation with another speaker;
- community/social interaction;
- media discussed with a speaker;
- voice messages;
- shared activity;
- online Egyptian social content used responsibly;
- practical interaction.

The long-term goal is for growth to become increasingly supported by **normal participation**, not permanent dependence on exercises.
