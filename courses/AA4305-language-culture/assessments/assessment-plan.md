# AA4305 Assessment Plan

## Assessment philosophy

AA4305 is assessed primarily through **evidence of learner-directed acquisition competence and real participation**, not through a large grammar/vocabulary final exam.

A strong student should be able to show:

`baseline → strategy/design → participation → evidence → revision → improved participation`

Use `resources/progress-measurement.md` for internal functional comparison. ACTFL concepts may inform descriptions, but this course does not issue official ACTFL ratings.

---

# 1. Baseline portfolio — prerequisite evidence

Complete before substantial study begins.

Include unrehearsed, comparable samples where feasible:

- spontaneous social conversation/self-introduction;
- picture/scene description;
- short listening comprehension task;
- narrative/retell;
- practical problem-solving interaction;
- at least one naturally occurring communication breakdown;
- reading/writing only if these are active learner goals;
- needs/strategy inventory;
- willingness-to-communicate opportunity log for the first week.

Score using the internal functional rubric but do **not** assign a proficiency label.

The baseline is not graded for language quality. It is graded for completeness/authenticity.

---

# 2. Acquisition-lab participation and design — 25%

Minimum **24 documented hours**.

Grade the learner, not the language partner.

### Rubric

- **session design/learner direction — 25%**
- **quality of meaningful interaction — 20%**
- **appropriate challenge/comprehensibility — 15%**
- **use of repair and interaction strategies — 15%**
- **reflection/adaptation between sessions — 15%**
- **ethical/privacy/relationship handling — 10%**

A session is not automatically excellent because it was conducted 100% in Arabic; incomprehensible interaction can be inefficient. Likewise, limited strategic English does not automatically invalidate a lab.

---

# 3. Learning journal and strategy experiments — 15%

Minimum **six explicit strategy experiments**.

Use:

`hypothesis → method → comparison/evidence → result → keep/modify/drop → next test`

At least one experiment each should address several of these areas:

- listening;
- vocabulary/chunks;
- feedback/correction;
- fluency;
- form/pronunciation;
- real-world participation/WTC.

### Grading

Reward experimental quality and evidence, not whether the preferred strategy “won.” Discovering that a favorite method is ineffective is a successful learning result.

---

# 4. Listening/speaking functional portfolio — 15%

Include baseline/mid/final comparable samples plus selected natural-task evidence.

Evaluate changes in:

- functions/tasks accomplished;
- comprehensibility;
- contexts/topics handled;
- text connectedness;
- listening comprehension;
- interaction/repair;
- familiar-task fluency;
- English rescue/dependence;
- willingness to continue communicating.

Use the internal rubric in `resources/progress-measurement.md`.

A rehearsed speech cannot substitute for spontaneous evidence.

---

# 5. Culture-learning / participant-observation portfolio — 15%

Minimum:

- **2 substantial culture-learning case studies**;
- ongoing culture hypothesis ledger;
- at least one documented misunderstanding/surprise/revision if one naturally occurs;
- evidence of seeking host perspective and variation where appropriate.

Grade:

- concrete observation;
- separation of observation vs. interpretation;
- multiple possible explanations;
- respectful host-perspective seeking;
- counterexamples/variation;
- revision of learner assumptions;
- practical change in future participation;
- privacy/ethical discipline.

Do not reward stereotype lists or copied “Egyptian culture facts.”

---

# 6. Reading/theory-to-practice responses — 10%

Readings are assessed through **application**, not summaries alone.

For substantial sources answer:

1. What is the claim?
2. What evidence supports it?
3. What does it predict I should do differently?
4. How did I test/apply it?
5. What happened?
6. What limitation prevents overgeneralization?

Required domains across the course:

- SLA explanation/input/interaction;
- Four Strands;
- vocabulary/multiword units;
- WTC/corrective feedback;
- culture observation;
- functional assessment;
- AI/CALL research.

---

# 7. Mid-course strategy + Four-Strands audit — 5%

Week 6.

Required:

- repeat selected baseline tasks;
- summarize hours by Four Strands;
- compare planned vs. actual participation;
- identify avoidance/WTC patterns;
- summarize strategy experiments;
- evaluate feedback relationship;
- identify culture-learning quality;
- create `acquisition-plan-v2`.

Use explicit:

`KEEP | MODIFY | DROP | ADD`

for major activities/strategies.

---

# 8. AI language-coach experiment — 5%

Evaluate AI as a **supplement to human participation**, not as the course authority.

Required dimensions:

- Egyptian Arabic vs. MSA/other-dialect control;
- linguistic accuracy;
- naturalness;
- register;
- correction usefulness;
- explanation quality;
- cultural-claim reliability;
- speech/pronunciation feedback where applicable;
- uncertainty/confidence calibration;
- effect on real-world human participation;
- validation burden placed on the native speaker.

Freeze experiment design before reading Week 11 outcome literature.

---

# 9. Final portfolio + oral defense — 10%

## Final evidence

Repeat comparable baseline tasks without rehearsal.

Include:

- baseline/mid/final rubric comparison;
- representative interaction excerpts/notes;
- final WTC/opportunity evidence;
- final Four-Strands audit;
- lexical/chunk evidence sample;
- strategy experiment synthesis;
- culture-learning synthesis;
- AI-use policy;
- 6–12 month continuation plan.

## Oral defense

30–45 minutes, recorded if practical.

Without reading a prepared essay, explain:

1. What can you now do that you could not do at baseline?
2. Which evidence is strongest?
3. Which strategies were overrated or ineffective?
4. How did your use of the native-speaker relationship change?
5. How did your handling of cultural difference mature?
6. Where did prior linguistic analysis help—and where did it get in the way?
7. Where does AI help and where is human participation indispensable?
8. What will your next 6–12 months actually look like?

---

# Mastery criteria

Target **85% overall** plus all non-negotiable conditions:

- 24+ legitimate acquisition-lab hours;
- genuine unrehearsed baseline/final evidence;
- six strategy experiments;
- demonstrated communication-repair ability;
- balanced-enough acquisition plan or documented correction of imbalance;
- culture portfolio showing revision rather than stereotyping;
- sustainable continuation system.

No particular unofficial proficiency label is required.

A learner can make modest linguistic gains and still demonstrate strong AA4305 competence if they clearly learn how to **diagnose, design, test, and improve** an acquisition system. Conversely, a learner who already speaks relatively well but cannot direct future learning has not fully met the course objective.
