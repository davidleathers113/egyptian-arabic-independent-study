# AA4305 — 12-Week Detailed Schedule

Target: **135 hours total** (~11.25 hours/week), including at least **24 hours of acquisition-lab/native-speaker participation**.

This schedule is competency-driven. If an activity is too easy or too advanced for the learner's current Egyptian Arabic, preserve the week's learning objective while changing the task difficulty.

## Weekly balance target

Use Paul Nation's Four Strands as an audit across the course:

- meaning-focused input;
- meaning-focused output;
- language-focused learning;
- fluency development.

Do not force exact 25% balance inside every week; correct persistent imbalance across multiple weeks.

---

## Week 1 — Baseline, needs analysis, and learner direction

### Academic work
- DIU AA4305 public description.
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 2, selected sections.
- ACTFL 2024 overview/FACT dimensions.
- GPA official overview.

### Practicum
- 2 hours with coach/partner.
- Record comparable, unrehearsed baseline tasks where consent permits:
  - conversation/self-introduction;
  - picture description;
  - listening comprehension;
  - narrative/retell if feasible;
  - interaction containing at least one natural breakdown.

### Planning
Inventory:
- situations in which Egyptian Arabic matters personally;
- current exposure/interaction opportunities;
- strongest/weakest skills;
- avoidance patterns;
- available weekly time;
- relationship/community opportunities;
- reading/writing goals if relevant.

### Outputs
- baseline portfolio;
- needs analysis;
- 12-week acquisition plan v1;
- functional task list;
- initial strategy hypotheses.

---

## Week 2 — Comprehensible participation and meaning-focused input

### Academic work
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 4, selective.
- Nation, “The Four Strands.”
- GPA activity guidance matched to current level.

### Practicum
Design highly contextualized interaction using some combination of:
- objects/actions;
- pictures/scenes;
- choices/contrasts;
- known people/events;
- demonstrated actions;
- shared experiences.

Avoid solving every unknown item with English translation.

### Independent practice
- replay selected coach recordings for meaning;
- use level-appropriate Egyptian Arabic audio;
- track comprehension by gist/details rather than transcript completeness.

### Outputs
- comprehensibility analysis;
- Four-Strands log #1;
- short reflection: `what made difficult speech learnable?`

---

## Week 3 — Listening systems and repeated encounters

### Academic work
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 2: learner language/vocabulary/pragmatics/pronunciation sections.
- Nation Ch. 4, selectively.
- GPA guidance on recordings/revisiting interactions.

### Practicum
Ask the coach for short natural material suitable for repeated listening:
- story;
- description;
- explanation;
- recount of a known event.

### Strategy experiment
Compare two or more:
- replay without text;
- replay with picture/context support;
- comprehension questions;
- retell;
- delayed transcript/gloss;
- chunk-focused replay.

### Evidence
Track:
- gist comprehension;
- detail recovery;
- segmentation/chunk recognition;
- speed tolerance;
- phrases noticed after repetition.

### Output
Repeated-listening experiment report + saved comparison sample.

---

## Week 4 — Interaction, repair, feedback, and willingness to communicate

### Academic work
- Lightbown & Spada Chs. 5–6 sections on interaction/corrective feedback.
- Peng (2025), selected WTC reading.
- Oliver & Adams (2021), selected oral corrective-feedback overview.

### Practicum
Practice repair as a core communicative competence:
- ask for repetition;
- ask for rephrasing;
- confirm understanding;
- signal partial understanding;
- request an example;
- keep the conversation alive after failure.

### Feedback experiment
Use two comparable sessions/tasks with different negotiated correction policies.

Example:
- Session A: recasts/communication-first.
- Session B: delayed explicit correction of 1–2 targets.

Compare what transfers into later spontaneous use.

### Outputs
- repair-strategy inventory;
- correction-policy v1;
- WTC/avoidance reflection;
- strategy experiment #2.

---

## Week 5 — Vocabulary, multiword units, and usable retrieval

### Academic work
- Nation Chs. 2, 4, 8, 12 selectively.

### Practicum
Collect words/phrases from actual interactions rather than creating a generic frequency list alone.

For selected items capture:
- spoken form;
- meaning/range;
- recurring frame/chunk;
- common collocation;
- grammatical behavior when relevant;
- social/register constraint;
- example from lived interaction.

### Strategy experiment
Compare two methods such as:
- isolated word cards vs. phrase/context cards;
- deliberate retrieval vs. repeated story exposure;
- recognition-only vs. productive recall;
- learner-created audio review vs. text review.

### Outcome measure
Test **delayed spontaneous retrieval/use**, not only same-day flashcard accuracy.

### Output
Personal lexical-system specification + strategy experiment #3.

---

## Week 6 — Individual differences, pronunciation triage, and mid-course audit

### Academic work
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 3.
- Peng, selected WTC factors.
- Nation Four Strands revisit.

### Practicum
Repeat several Week 1 tasks with comparable difficulty.

### Pronunciation/perception triage
Use AL4302/AL4303 knowledge to identify only high-value issues that:
- repeatedly cause misunderstanding;
- prevent word recognition;
- create important lexical/grammatical confusion;
- materially reduce confidence/willingness to speak.

Choose 1–3 targets, not a full accent-removal project.

### Mid-course audit
Review:
- hours and interaction quality;
- Four-Strands balance;
- listening growth;
- output growth;
- repair behavior;
- WTC/avoidance;
- vocabulary retrieval;
- coach feedback mode;
- culture observations;
- strategy experiments.

### Output
- acquisition plan v2;
- mid-course evidence comparison;
- explicit `keep / modify / drop` strategy table.

---

## Week 7 — Grammar discovery without grammar domination

### Academic work
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 6, selected proposals on comprehension, interaction and form-focus.
- selective language-focused-learning reference.

### Practicum
Choose one recurring form/construction already appearing naturally in conversation.

Cycle:
1. notice;
2. collect examples;
3. infer provisional form-function pattern;
4. request limited clarification/examples;
5. design a meaning-focused activity that naturally uses it;
6. return to spontaneous interaction;
7. see whether access improves later.

### Constraint
No long AL4410-style analysis during the lab. Formal analysis belongs in private review after interaction.

### Output
Form-function discovery memo + strategy experiment #4.

---

## Week 8 — Stories, connected discourse, and fluency

### Academic work
- Nation Four Strands: fluency development.
- GPA story/retell guidance appropriate to current level.
- Nation multiword-unit material if useful.

### Practicum
Use:
- picture sequence;
- coach story + comprehension;
- learner retell;
- personal story told more than once;
- known-topic conversation repeated with changed partner/task conditions where possible.

### Fluency experiment
Repeat a mostly familiar task at separated intervals. Track:
- hesitation/pausing;
- retrieval speed;
- connectedness/text length;
- repair frequency;
- ability to elaborate;
- comprehensibility.

Do not equate speed with good communication.

### Output
Narrative/fluency portfolio + strategy experiment #5.

---

## Week 9 — Culture acquisition through participant observation

### Academic work
- Spradley: ethnography/culture, ethnographic record, descriptive observation, domain analysis.
- GPA host-world participation perspective.

### Practicum/lifestyle participation
Choose one recurring cultural domain actually encountered, for example:
- hospitality;
- invitations;
- greetings/farewells;
- requests/favors;
- family/social talk;
- politeness/respect;
- disagreement;
- humor;
- time/planning;
- religious/social expressions.

### Culture-learning cycle
`concrete observation → fieldnote → interpretation hypothesis → host perspective → contrast/counterexample → revision`

Seek multiple examples/perspectives where practical.

### Output
Culture-learning case study #1 + hypothesis log.

---

## Week 10 — Real-world tasks and lifestyle participation

### Academic work
- ACTFL FACT framework/descriptors appropriate to current ability.
- GPA special-growth vs. lifestyle participation.
- Spradley focused observation selectively.

### Practicum
Complete authentic or realistic functions selected from actual goals:
- arrange plans;
- explain a problem;
- ask/follow up for information;
- narrate what happened;
- give reasons/preferences;
- negotiate a misunderstanding;
- participate in a culturally meaningful topic.

### Assessment
Use the course functional-performance rubric:
- task accomplishment;
- comprehensibility;
- interaction/repair;
- text connectedness;
- range;
- accuracy that materially affects communication;
- reliance on English/help.

### Output
Real-world task packet + culture-learning case study #2 + strategy experiment #6.

---

## Week 11 — AI language-coach experiment

### Before research reading
Freeze:
- AI tools/tasks being tested;
- prompt/procedure;
- scoring criteria;
- native-speaker validation questions.

### Then read
- Goh & Aryadoust (2025) on AI for L2 listening/speaking.
- Hou & Min (2026) dialogue-based CALL meta-analysis.
- Liu & Zhao (2026) AI-mediated informal language-learning scoping review.
- Wang & Zhang (2026) GenAI-supported self-regulated informal learning, selectively.

### Benchmark domains
Test 4–6:
- role-play;
- correction;
- naturalness/register;
- Egyptian-vs-MSA/dialect control;
- vocabulary/chunk suggestions;
- colloquial grammar explanation;
- pronunciation feedback where supported;
- story/dialogue generation;
- culture advice.

### Native-speaker validation
Use selected examples only; do not turn the consultant into a full-time AI fact checker.

### Output
AI language-coach evaluation + personal AI-use policy.

---

## Week 12 — Final growth evidence, myth audit, and continuation plan

### Academic work
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 7.
- ACTFL overview/descriptors revisit.
- Nation Four Strands final audit.
- GPA long-term participation vision selectively.

### Final evidence
Repeat comparable Week 1 tasks **unrehearsed**.

Compare:
- functions/tasks handled;
- comprehensibility;
- listening comprehension;
- text length/connectedness;
- interaction/repair;
- familiar-task fluency;
- lexical/chunk retrieval;
- willingness to communicate;
- independence from English;
- ability to learn from native-speaker interaction;
- culture-learning maturity.

### Final reflection
Answer:
- Which strategies produced observable gains?
- Which consumed time without enough return?
- Which difficulties are linguistic vs. interactional vs. affective vs. logistical?
- Which activities should become weekly habits?
- What needs formal instruction vs. continued participation?
- How will real Egyptian Arabic relationships/communities remain central?

### Outputs
- final portfolio;
- 6–12 month continuation plan;
- final Four-Strands design;
- oral defense/reflection.

# Typical weekly hour allocation

The exact pattern may vary, but a useful default is:

| Activity | Hours |
|---|---:|
| Acquisition lab / native-speaker participation | 2.0 |
| Meaning-focused independent listening | 1.75 |
| Meaning-focused output / real-world use | 1.25 |
| Fluency development | 1.0 |
| Language-focused learning (vocab/form/pronunciation) | 1.5 |
| Academic reading/research | 1.5 |
| Culture observation/participation | 1.0 |
| Journal/portfolio/strategy analysis | 1.25 |
| **Total** | **11.25** |

This table separates activity types for planning; real activities may serve more than one function. Avoid double-counting the same clock time in the hours log.
