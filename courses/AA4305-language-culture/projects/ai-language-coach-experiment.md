# Project — AI as an Egyptian Arabic Language Coach

## Research question

Where can AI productively support learner-directed Egyptian Arabic acquisition, and where does it diverge from native-speaker language, cultural judgment, or relational participation?

## Test domains

Choose 4–6:
- role-play/conversation practice;
- correction of learner-produced Egyptian Arabic;
- naturalness/register judgments;
- vocabulary/phrase suggestions;
- explanation of colloquial constructions;
- pronunciation/speech feedback where supported;
- story or dialogue generation;
- cultural-scenario advice.

## Procedure

1. Define the learner task and success criterion.
2. Use AI for the task and preserve the output.
3. Independently decide what seems useful/questionable.
4. Ask the native speaker to judge selected language examples for naturalness, meaning, register, and appropriateness.
5. Record disagreements without pressuring the consultant to validate the AI.
6. Where possible, seek more than one native-speaker/source perspective for culture-heavy claims.

## Evaluation dimensions

- linguistic accuracy;
- Egyptian vs. MSA/other-dialect drift;
- naturalness;
- register;
- cultural/contextual appropriateness;
- usefulness to the learner;
- quality of explanations;
- confidence calibration;
- invented or overgeneralized cultural claims;
- ability to support actual participation rather than only simulated conversation.

## Final report

2,500–4,000 words plus examples/scoring tables. Conclude with a practical personal policy for using AI during ongoing Egyptian Arabic acquisition and with implications for language-technology tools used in cross-cultural or Bible-translation settings.
