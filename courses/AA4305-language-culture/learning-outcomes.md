# AA4305 Learning Outcomes

By the end of the study, the student will be able to:

1. Conduct a realistic language/culture **needs analysis** grounded in actual situations and relationships.
2. Set functional goals describing what the learner needs to understand/do rather than merely which grammar/vocabulary topics to cover.
3. Design learner-directed native-speaker sessions without requiring the partner to be a trained teacher.
4. Distinguish productive roles for **teacher mode**, **acquisition-lab/nurturer mode**, and **normal lifestyle participation**.
5. Use context, objects, pictures, actions, shared experiences, stories, and interaction to make unfamiliar speech learnable without constant translation.
6. Balance a longer-term acquisition program across meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused learning, and fluency development using Nation's Four Strands as an audit framework.
7. Build repeated-listening systems and evaluate growth in gist, detail, segmentation/chunk recognition, and natural-speed tolerance.
8. Use communication-repair strategies spontaneously to sustain interaction through misunderstanding.
9. Negotiate and test different corrective-feedback arrangements with a native speaker rather than assuming every error should be corrected immediately.
10. Track willingness-to-communicate/avoidance as a context-sensitive behavior and deliberately expand real participation.
11. Build vocabulary and multiword-unit systems tied to spoken form, context, collocation, register, and delayed spontaneous retrieval rather than translation pairs alone.
12. Diagnose selected persistent pronunciation/perception problems using prior phonetic/phonological training when they materially affect communication.
13. Notice recurring grammatical form-function patterns and use prior grammatical-analysis skill selectively without turning interaction into constant analysis.
14. Develop fluency through repeated work on mostly familiar language/content and distinguish fluency development from simply speaking faster.
15. Observe cultural behavior concretely before interpreting it.
16. Maintain a culture-hypothesis ledger distinguishing observation, learner interpretation, host perspective, counterevidence, and confidence/generalizability.
17. Seek multiple perspectives and revise cultural interpretations rather than treating one speaker as a representative of all Egyptians.
18. Complete practical real-world communication tasks and evaluate them with repeatable functional criteria.
19. Document growth through comparable unrehearsed baseline, mid-course, and final tasks using ACTFL FACT concepts responsibly without claiming an official ACTFL rating.
20. Run at least six explicit learning-strategy experiments using `hypothesis → method → evidence → keep/modify/drop`.
21. Evaluate AI language-learning tools for Egyptian-dialect accuracy, naturalness, register, cultural reliability, corrective value, and transfer to real human participation.
22. Protect native-speaker relationships and privacy while collecting enough evidence to support learning claims.
23. Produce and defend a sustainable 6–12 month post-course Egyptian Arabic acquisition plan.
24. Explain how the skills learned in AA4305 transfer to future field-language situations where commercial curricula, trained teachers, or abundant resources may not exist.

## Evidence requirement

Outcomes involving communication, culture, strategy effectiveness, or learner autonomy must be supported by participation/portfolio evidence, not only essays about theory.

A strong final portfolio shows not simply that Egyptian Arabic improved, but that the learner became **better at engineering future learning through people, participation, observation, and evidence**.
