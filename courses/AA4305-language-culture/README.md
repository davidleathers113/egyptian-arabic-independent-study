# AA4305 Independent Study — Second Language and Culture Acquisition

**Modeled DIU course:** AA4305 Second Language and Culture Acquisition  
**Modeled credits:** 3  
**Target workload:** 135 documented hours  
**Primary language:** Egyptian Arabic

## Purpose

Develop a deliberate, learner-directed approach to acquiring language and culture through participation with native speakers, reflection, strategy design, and measurable growth in spontaneous communication.

DIU publicly describes AA4305 as learner-directed second language and culture acquisition with emphasis on beginning stages, perspectives on language/culture learning, strategies for cultural differences, and a practical lab in a small-group, non-instructed setting with a native speaker.

## Special design for this project

The existing Egyptian Arabic coach relationship is ideal, but the independent-study practicum must distinguish two modes:

1. **Normal coaching/tutoring** — the coach may explain, teach, correct, translate, or structure lessons.
2. **Acquisition lab / nurturer mode** — the coach participates as a native speaker and communication partner while the student directs activities designed to make meaning comprehensible and progressively increase participation.

Only the second mode is counted as the core analogue to DIU's non-instructed native-speaker lab, though ordinary coaching may support the wider 135-hour study.

## Major outputs

1. Baseline language/culture profile.
2. Personal language-learning plan and strategy revisions.
3. Documented acquisition-lab sessions.
4. Listening and speaking growth portfolio.
5. Culture-observation/participation journal.
6. Comprehensible-input and interaction projects.
7. Learner-created activity library.
8. Mid-course strategy audit.
9. AI language-coach experiment.
10. Final proficiency/growth evidence and reflective portfolio.

## Mastery standard

The course is not passed by memorizing vocabulary or explaining SLA theory. Mastery requires evidence that the student can design, execute, evaluate, and revise a learner-directed acquisition program while demonstrating real growth in spontaneous Egyptian Arabic participation and cross-cultural competence.
