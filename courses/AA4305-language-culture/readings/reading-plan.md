# AA4305 Detailed Reading and Resource Plan

## How to use this plan

AA4305 is a **practice-led** course. Readings exist to improve actual language/culture acquisition decisions. If reading begins displacing meaningful Egyptian Arabic participation, the curriculum is out of balance.

Each resource has a defined job:

- **Lightbown & Spada** = academic SLA backbone and research reality-check.
- **Growing Participator Approach (GPA)** = practical relational/participatory field-learning model.
- **Nation's Four Strands** = course-balance audit.
- **Nation, _Learning Vocabulary in Another Language_** = selective vocabulary/multiword-unit/autonomy reference.
- **ACTFL 2024** = functional performance descriptors, never a self-issued official rating.
- **Spradley** = culture-learning observation/fieldnote method.
- **Peng on willingness to communicate** = current research on turning language knowledge into actual communication.
- **Corrective-feedback research** = basis for negotiating useful correction with a language partner.
- **AUC Press Kallimni ‘Arabi series** = optional level-appropriate Egyptian Arabic structured input/activity/audio bank.
- **2025–2026 AI/CALL research** = Week 11 research context after the experiment design is frozen.

Do not read every source cover-to-cover. Record exact pages from the editions actually used.

# Core academic book

## Patsy M. Lightbown & Nina Spada — _How Languages Are Learned_, 5th ed. (2021)

Use primarily:

1. **Ch. 2 — Second language learning**
   - learner characteristics/conditions;
   - learner language;
   - developmental sequences;
   - first-language influence;
   - vocabulary, pragmatics, pronunciation;
   - ways of sampling learner language.

2. **Ch. 3 — Individual differences in second language learning**
   - motivation/affect/aptitude and other individual variation;
   - use critically: individual differences inform strategy experiments but do not justify fixed learner labels.

3. **Ch. 4 — Explaining second language learning**
   - compare major explanatory perspectives;
   - focus on what each theory predicts learners need to experience/do.

4. **Ch. 5 — Observing learning and teaching**
   - natural vs. instructional settings;
   - interaction patterns;
   - corrective feedback;
   - observation/ethnographic awareness.

5. **Ch. 6 — Second language learning in the classroom**
   - compare proposals emphasizing accuracy, comprehension, interaction, content, teachability, and form-focus.
   - translate classroom findings cautiously into a self-directed/field setting.

6. **Ch. 7 — Popular ideas about language learning revisited**
   - final myth/assumption audit.

Chapter 1 (first-language acquisition) is background only unless a specific question requires it.

# Practicum framework

## Growing Participator Approach — official materials

Read/view the official overview and only those phase materials that match the learner's actual level and course tasks.

Key concepts:

- language/culture learned as participation rather than a detached code;
- nurturer/host relationships;
- special-growth participation vs. lifestyle participation;
- comprehension-rich activity design;
- recordings made for future growth;
- increasing complexity and relational depth;
- eventual movement away from artificial learning sessions toward normal host-world participation.

### Important scope control

The official GPA model describes a six-phase growth program involving far more than 135 hours. Use its **principles and activities** without claiming this course completes a GPA phase unless the actual hours/criteria independently justify that claim.

If the learner already has meaningful Egyptian Arabic ability, enter at the **activity level that creates useful challenge** rather than artificially recreating absolute-beginner Phase 1.

# Curriculum-balance framework

## Paul Nation — “The Four Strands”

Read the full article early in the course.

Four strands:

1. meaning-focused input;
2. meaning-focused output;
3. language-focused learning;
4. fluency development.

Use the framework for weekly and mid-course audits. Rough balance matters across the course; this is not a requirement that each 60-minute session contain exactly 15 minutes of each strand.

# Culture-learning method

## James P. Spradley — _Participant Observation_

Use selected portions:

### Part One
- ethnography and culture;
- ethnographic research cycle.

### Developmental Research Sequence — adapted
- locate a social situation/domain;
- make an ethnographic record;
- descriptive observation;
- domain analysis;
- focused observation;
- taxonomic/componential thinking as useful;
- cultural themes only after substantial evidence.

### Adaptation rule

This independent study is **learner culture acquisition**, not formal human-subject ethnography. Apply Spradley's discipline to your own observation and interpretation while protecting relationships, privacy, and dignity. Do not secretly analyze private personal disclosures for public academic artifacts.

# Week-by-week research/readings

## Week 1 — Baseline, learner direction, and what acquisition evidence looks like

### Required
- DIU AA4305 public course description.
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 2, selected sections on learner characteristics/conditions, learner language, and sampling learner language.
- ACTFL 2024 overview (FACT framework).
- GPA official “Our Approach” / program overview.

### Read/reflect
Ask:
- What can I actually do spontaneously now?
- What language knowledge do I possess but fail to access in interaction?
- In which situations do I most need Egyptian Arabic?
- Which tasks would constitute meaningful progress 12 weeks from now?

### Output
Baseline portfolio + acquisition plan v1 + task inventory.

---

## Week 2 — Designing comprehensible, meaning-focused participation

### Required
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 4, selectively: compare explanatory perspectives on input/interaction/learning.
- Paul Nation, “The Four Strands.”
- GPA phase/activity guidance appropriate to current learner ability.

### Practice connection
Design nurturer sessions in which meaning is recoverable through shared context, actions, objects, pictures, previously understood material, or naturally constrained situations.

### Research question
What makes input **comprehensible enough to learn from without making it trivial**?

### Output
Meaning-focused-input design memo + first Four-Strands time log.

---

## Week 3 — Listening, repeated encounters, and learner-language evidence

### Required
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 2 sections on learner language, vocabulary, pragmatics, and pronunciation.
- Nation, _Learning Vocabulary_, Ch. 4 (vocabulary in listening/speaking), selectively.
- GPA guidance on recordings/revisiting participation material.

### Optional Egyptian Arabic resource
Use level-appropriate Kallimni ‘Arabi native-speaker audio as **additional controlled listening**, not as the only listening source.

### Experiment
Compare at least two repeated-listening methods, such as:
- repeated listening with no text;
- picture/context support;
- delayed transcript/gloss after meaning attempts;
- retelling/checking with coach.

### Output
Listening strategy experiment + evidence of what improved (gist, details, segmentation, speed tolerance, etc.).

---

## Week 4 — Interaction, repair, corrective feedback, and willingness to communicate

### Required
- Lightbown & Spada Chs. 5–6 sections on interaction and corrective feedback.
- Jian-E Peng, _Willingness to Communicate in a Second Language_ (2025), introduction + selected sections relevant to out-of-class/dynamic/contextual willingness to communicate.
- Oliver & Adams, “Oral Corrective Feedback” (2021), selected overview sections.

### Practice
Build/automate Egyptian Arabic repair moves for:
- asking for repetition;
- asking someone to slow or rephrase;
- checking interpretation;
- signaling partial understanding;
- confirming a word/phrase;
- continuing after a breakdown.

### Feedback experiment
Compare two negotiated feedback modes across similar activities. Examples:
- recasts during interaction vs. delayed explicit feedback;
- correction of one target feature vs. broad correction.

### Output
Communication-repair inventory + correction-policy v1 + WTC reflection.

---

## Week 5 — Vocabulary, chunks, and retrieval for real life

### Required
- Nation Ch. 2 — what it means to know a word.
- Nation Ch. 4 — listening/speaking.
- Nation Ch. 8 — strategies/autonomy.
- Nation Ch. 12 — multiword units.

### Principles to test
A usable lexical item includes more than translation:
- spoken form;
- meaning/range;
- grammatical behavior;
- collocations;
- register/social constraints;
- common multiword frames;
- ability to recognize and retrieve it rapidly.

### Strategy experiment
Compare two systems (for example isolated flashcards vs. phrase/context cards, or deliberate review vs. repeated story exposure) using later spontaneous retrieval as evidence.

### Output
Personal lexical-system specification + experiment report.

---

## Week 6 — Individual differences, affect, pronunciation support, and mid-course redesign

### Required
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 3.
- Peng (2025), selected factors affecting willingness to communicate.
- revisit Nation's Four Strands.

### Critical-reading rule
Do not convert “individual differences” into fixed labels such as “I am a visual learner, therefore…”. Treat preferences, motivation, anxiety, time constraints, and strategies as **hypotheses to test**.

### Pronunciation integration
Use AL4302/AL4303 knowledge only on persistent perception/production issues that materially interfere with communication or listening. Select a small number of high-value targets.

### Mid-course audit
- repeat selected baseline tasks;
- Four-Strands balance;
- WTC/avoidance patterns;
- acquisition-lab quality;
- strategy keep/modify/drop decisions;
- plan v2.

---

## Week 7 — Noticing form without turning life into a grammar exercise

### Required
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 6, especially the comparison of comprehension, interaction, teachability, and form-focused proposals.
- selected Nation material on language-focused learning if available.
- revisit AL4410 notes only when a recurring construction genuinely needs analysis.

### Practice cycle
1. encounter recurring form in meaningful interaction;
2. notice a pattern/problem;
3. collect a few natural examples;
4. form a provisional hypothesis;
5. ask/confirm only what is needed;
6. immediately return to meaning-focused use;
7. observe whether the form becomes more available later.

### Output
Form-function discovery memo based on one naturally recurring Egyptian Arabic construction.

---

## Week 8 — Narratives, connected speech, and fluency development

### Required
- revisit Nation's fluency-development strand.
- GPA materials appropriate to picture stories, shared stories, retelling, or later-phase narrative participation.
- Nation Ch. 12 if formulaic/multiword units emerge strongly in narratives.

### Fluency rule
Fluency tasks should use **mostly familiar language/content** and reduce processing pressure through repetition, prior context, or repeated task performance. New difficult content belongs mainly elsewhere.

### Practice
- picture-sequence story;
- coach story + learner retell;
- same personal story at multiple time points;
- familiar-topic timed/repeated conversation without sacrificing comprehensibility.

### Output
Narrative/fluency portfolio with comparable samples.

---

## Week 9 — Culture acquisition through disciplined participation

### Required
- Spradley Part One + steps on ethnographic record, descriptive observation, and domain analysis.
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 5 section(s) relevant to naturalistic observation/ethnography, if available in the edition used.
- GPA material on host-world participation/culture inseparability.

### Method
For a selected recurring social domain (for example hospitality, requests, greetings, disagreement, family talk, time, humor, service encounters):

`observe → write concrete fieldnote → propose cultural domain/hypothesis → ask host perspective → seek contrast/counterexample → revise`

### Output
Culture-learning case study #1 with explicit uncertainty and multiple perspectives where possible.

---

## Week 10 — Lifestyle participation and functional real-world tasks

### Required
- ACTFL relevant speaking/listening descriptors; use FACT to design tasks.
- GPA distinction between special-growth participation and lifestyle participation.
- Spradley focused observation material selectively.

### Task design
Choose authentic or realistic goals such as:
- arranging plans;
- telling what happened;
- getting/clarifying information;
- explaining a problem;
- expressing preferences/reasons;
- asking follow-up questions;
- handling a misunderstanding;
- participating in a culturally meaningful conversation domain.

### Evidence
Score task performance using a **course-created functional rubric**, not an official ACTFL rating.

### Output
Real-world task packet + fieldnote + breakdown/repair analysis.

---

## Week 11 — AI-mediated language learning: useful practice, weak authority

### Experiment-design rule
Freeze the experimental tasks and evaluation rubric **before** reading the outcome literature below.

### Required after design freeze
- Goh & Aryadoust (2025), “Developing and assessing second language listening and speaking: Does AI make it better?”
- Hou & Min (2026), meta-analysis of dialogue-based CALL for L2 speaking.
- Liu & Zhao (2026), scoping review of AI-mediated informal language learning.
- Wang & Zhang (2026), GenAI-supported self-regulated informal language learning, selectively.

### Research purpose
Do not ask “Is AI good for language learning?” Ask which tasks, under which conditions, for which learner goals, with what validation burden.

### Egyptian Arabic benchmark
Compare AI with native-speaker evidence for:
- Egyptian vs. MSA/other-dialect drift;
- naturalness;
- register;
- correction quality;
- role-play usefulness;
- cultural claims;
- pronunciation/speech judgments if supported;
- whether AI increases **actual human communication** or merely simulated practice.

### Output
AI language-coach report + personal AI-use policy.

---

## Week 12 — Evidence synthesis, myths, and continuation design

### Required
- Lightbown & Spada Ch. 7 — popular language-learning ideas revisited.
- ACTFL overview/descriptors again.
- Nation Four Strands again.
- GPA long-term participation vision selectively.

### Final synthesis
Repeat comparable unrehearsed baseline tasks and compare:
- functions/tasks;
- accuracy where it affects communication;
- contexts/topics handled;
- text length/connectedness;
- comprehension;
- repair;
- speed/fluency on familiar material;
- willingness to communicate;
- ability to learn through interaction;
- quality of culture-learning observation.

### Output
Final portfolio + 6–12 month acquisition plan + oral defense.

# Egyptian Arabic structured resource options

The Kallimni ‘Arabi series should be selected by **actual current ability**:

- _Kallimni ‘Arabi Bishweesh_ — beginner;
- _Kallimni ‘Arabi_ — intermediate;
- _Kallimni ‘Arabi Aktar_ — upper intermediate;
- later volumes — advanced.

AUC Press provides native-speaker audio for the series. Use selected dialogues/tasks as additional input, listening practice, or prompts for real coach interaction.

Do not let a coursebook replace learner-directed work. If a module is irrelevant to actual communication needs, skip it.

# Reading response standard

For academic readings, record:

1. full citation;
2. central claim;
3. evidence/research design used;
4. learner behavior the claim predicts should help/hurt;
5. important limitation/context boundary;
6. one Egyptian Arabic acquisition experiment suggested by the reading;
7. what evidence would make you reject/modify the strategy;
8. outcome after testing it.

For culture readings add:

9. risk of overgeneralization;
10. how host perspectives will be sought ethically.

For AI readings add:

11. exact technology/task studied;
12. whether evidence transfers plausibly to Egyptian Arabic and to your use case;
13. human/native-speaker validation still required.
