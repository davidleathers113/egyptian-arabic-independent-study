# AL4303 — 12-Week Executable Schedule

Target: **135 hours total** (~11.25 hours/week). Adjust individual weeks as needed while preserving the total and required outputs.

Abbreviations:
- **B** = Burquest, _Phonological Analysis_, 3rd ed.
- **UP** = Gussenhoven & Jacobs, _Understanding Phonology_, 5th ed.
- **EOL** = _Essentials of Linguistics_, 2nd ed., Ch. 4.
- **MIT** = MIT OCW 24.961 Introduction to Phonology.
- **Snider** = _Tone Analysis for Field Linguists_.

## Week 1 — From phones to phonological categories

**Read**
- B 1.1–1.2; 2.1.
- EOL 4.1.
- UP Ch. 1 selectively.

**Analyze**
- Review inherited AL4302 corpus without importing a published Egyptian Arabic phoneme inventory.
- Complete introductory phonemic-analysis practice.
- Build Egyptian Arabic hypothesis ledger: `observation → possible analysis → evidence needed`.

**Output**
- baseline phonological analysis of 20–30 inherited tokens, explicitly provisional.

---

## Week 2 — Contrast and phonemic procedure

**Read**
- B 2.2–2.5.
- EOL 4.3, 4.5, 4.6.
- UP 3.2–3.5.

**Analyze**
- contrast in identical/analogous environments;
- minimal/near-minimal evidence;
- system gaps and typological caution.

**Fieldwork**
- design elicitation to test 3–5 Egyptian Arabic candidate contrasts.

**Optional stretch**
- MIT contrast/perceptual-distinctiveness material after core analysis.

**Output**
- contrast table + consultant elicitation record.

---

## Week 3 — Complementary distribution, variation, phonotactics

**Read**
- B 2.6–2.7.
- EOL 4.2, 4.4.
- UP Ch. 4.

**Analyze**
- complementary vs. overlapping distribution;
- free variation;
- phonotactic restrictions;
- neutralization/underspecification only to introductory depth.

**Egyptian Arabic**
- reanalyze AL4302 variants without assuming they are allophones.

**Output**
- distributional analysis report with competing analyses where warranted.

---

## Week 4 — Distinctive features and natural classes

**Read**
- B 1.3.
- UP Ch. 5.
- revisit EOL 4.2.

**Analyze**
- feature matrices;
- natural-class formulations;
- redundant vs. contrastive properties;
- phonetic plausibility.

**Stretch**
- selected MIT feature-organization notes 10–12.

**Output**
- cross-linguistic feature packet + provisional Egyptian Arabic natural-class memo.

---

## Week 5 — Processes and conditioning environments

**Read**
- B 4.1–4.5.
- EOL 4.7–4.9.
- UP 6.1–6.2 and 7.5 selectively.

**Analyze**
- assimilation/dissimilation and other observed process types;
- environment notation;
- phonetic plausibility;
- process vs. phonetic implementation.

**Egyptian Arabic**
- test connected-speech observations and actively search for counterexamples.
- after freezing relevant analysis, read Youssef (2016), “Palatalization in educated Cairene Arabic.”
- if the corpus contains relevant obstruent clusters, Kabrah (2011) is an optional post-analysis comparison.

**Output**
- process-analysis packet + Youssef comparison memo.

---

## Week 6 — Syllables, phonotactics, neutralization

**Read**
- B 5.1–5.7; revisit 2.7.
- UP Ch. 8.

**Analyze**
- syllable constituents;
- sonority/sequence restrictions;
- ambiguous segments/sequences;
- length and weight;
- syllable-conditioned processes.

**Stretch**
- selected MIT syllable notes 19–21.
- inspect MIT Problem Set 4 (“Italian Stressed Syllables”) and assign only if the actual task fits the competency target.

**Egyptian Arabic**
- build a preliminary phonotactic/syllabification inventory from corpus data.
- after freezing it, consult Aquil (2012), “The Role of the Syllable in the Segmentation of Cairene Spoken Arabic.”

**Midterm practical**
- unseen datasets covering contrast, distribution, features, and processes.

**Output**
- syllable/phonotactic memo + midterm/error analysis.

---

## Week 7 — Stress and larger domains

**Read**
- B Ch. 6.
- B 8.1–8.5 selectively.
- UP Ch. 10–11.

**Analyze**
- word-level vs. utterance-level conditioning;
- stress/weight;
- prosodic domains;
- difference between acoustic prominence and phonological stress.

**Stretch**
- selected MIT stress notes 22–25.

**Egyptian Arabic**
- freeze corpus-based stress hypotheses first.
- then consult Hellmuth's Cairo Arabic word-stress work.
- optional: use Hellmuth's 2006 thesis to distinguish word stress from postlexical pitch accent and larger prosodic organization.

**Output**
- stress/domain analysis memo.

---

## Week 8 — Tone analysis practicum

**This week is deliberately non-Arabic.**

**Read**
- Snider Ch. 1.
- Snider Ch. 2.
- selected Snider Ch. 3 on phonetics of pitch.
- UP Ch. 9.
- B 7.1–7.2 at introductory depth.

**Lecture/resource**
- MIT Tone-1: African Languages.
- MIT Tone-2: Asian Languages.

**Analyze**
- one documented tone-language dataset;
- phonetic pitch vs. phonological tone;
- controlled/comparable environments;
- tonal contrast/patterns;
- introductory nonlinear representation where useful.

**Output**
- independent tone-analysis practical;
- correction/error report after legitimate feedback/solution access.

---

## Week 9 — Morphophonemics I

**Read**
- B 3.1–3.5.
- UP 4.4–4.5.
- UP 7.1–7.4 selectively.

**Analyze**
- phonologically conditioned alternations;
- lexical alternations;
- morphologically conditioned alternations;
- representation/rule choices.

**Egyptian Arabic**
- begin a paradigm-oriented alternation case study from actual data.

**Cross-linguistic requirement**
- complete at least one substantial non-Arabic morphophonemic problem.

**Output**
- alternation dataset + first analysis.

---

## Week 10 — Morphophonemics II and underlying representation

**Read**
- B 3.6–3.8.
- revisit UP 4.5 and 6.2.

**Analyze**
- competing underlying forms;
- evidence for/against abstractions;
- ordering/interactions where genuinely needed;
- falsification evidence.

**Egyptian Arabic**
- finish independent case analysis first.
- then read Xu (2025), “Non-concatenative morphological domains constrain phonotactics: a case study of Egyptian Arabic.”

**Output**
- 6–10 page morphophonemic case study;
- literature-comparison appendix;
- handoff questions for AL4410.

---

## Week 11 — Orthography implications and AI benchmark

**Read**
- Cahill (2014), “Non-linguistic factors in orthographies.”
- Snider (2014), “Orthography and phonological depth.”
- Cahill & Rice introduction if accessible.
- issue-specific tone/word-boundary chapter only if relevant.

**Orthography assignment**
- write one limited orthography decision memo;
- identify what phonology determines and what only users/stakeholders/testing can determine.

**AI benchmark**
- only after human grading, run selected unseen previously completed datasets through AI;
- score contrast, representation, process, evidence, uncertainty, missed alternatives, unsupported claims.

**Outputs**
- orthography memo;
- AI benchmark report.

---

## Week 12 — Synthesis

**Read**
- targeted Burquest rereading based on weak areas.
- revisit UP Ch. 3 and 7 where helpful.
- no new major theoretical unit.

**Final project**
- complete preliminary Egyptian Arabic phonological sketch;
- audit every major claim to tokens/evidence;
- mark unresolved issues explicitly;
- create next-elicitation list.

**Breadth audit**
- index all graded non-Arabic datasets by language/phenomenon/skill.

**Final practical**
- mixed unseen cross-linguistic datasets.

**Oral defense**
- defend 3–5 major analyses;
- explain at least one analysis you deliberately leave unresolved;
- identify what is being deferred to formal advanced phonology.
