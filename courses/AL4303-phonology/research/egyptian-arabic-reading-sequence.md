# Egyptian Arabic Research Sequence — AL4303

## Purpose

Egyptian Arabic is the longitudinal field language, but published descriptions must not become answer keys. This document sequences scholarship **after** the learner has created and frozen a relevant analysis from consultant/corpus data.

The goal is to practice the real scholarly cycle:

`primary data → provisional analysis → external scholarship → comparison → revision/defense`

not:

`read description → find examples that confirm it`.

---

# Universal pre-reading protocol

Before opening a source about the phenomenon under study, save a versioned memo containing:

1. dataset/session IDs;
2. observations;
3. distribution table;
4. proposed contrast/process/representation;
5. competing analysis if plausible;
6. counterexamples/exceptions;
7. confidence level;
8. elicitation still needed.

Suggested filename:

`EA-AL4303-<phenomenon>-analysis-v0-preliterature.md`

After reading the external source, create:

`EA-AL4303-<phenomenon>-literature-comparison-v1.md`

with:

- what the source claims;
- what data/evidence it uses;
- where it agrees with your analysis;
- where it disagrees;
- whether the speakers/varieties are actually comparable;
- what you changed;
- why you changed it;
- what remains unresolved.

Never erase the pre-literature analysis.

# Week 5 — Process vs. phonetic implementation

## Islam Youssef (2016)
**“Palatalization in educated Cairene Arabic.”** _Nordlyd_ 42(1): 21–31.

Open article:
https://septentrio.uit.no/index.php/nordlyd/article/view/3739

### Why this reading is unusually useful

The paper explicitly investigates whether observed secondary palatal articulation should be treated as phonetic coarticulation or phonological assimilation, while also considering possible phonologization in a different sociolect.

That makes it an ideal case study for the AL4303 distinction between:

- phonetic output;
- phonological category/process;
- sociolinguistic variation;
- sound change/phonologization.

### Before reading

Analyze any relevant coronal + high-front-vowel/glide data from SPK001 without assuming the pattern Youssef reports exists in your coach's variety.

### After reading

Write 750–1,250 words answering:

1. What evidence does Youssef use to call a pattern phonetic rather than phonological?
2. Which of those diagnostics can you apply to your corpus?
3. Are the sociolect/speaker populations comparable?
4. What would constitute evidence of phonologization in your speaker's variety?

# Week 5 optional — Regressive voicing assimilation

## Rawiah S. Kabrah (2011)
**“Regressive voicing assimilation in Cairene Arabic.”** In _Perspectives on Arabic Linguistics XXII–XXIII_, pp. 21–34.

Publisher record:
https://benjamins.com/catalog/cilt.317.02kab

### Use only if relevant data exist

The published abstract describes voicing agreement in obstruent sequences, with the first obstruent assimilating to the second, and discusses how gutturals pattern.

### Before reading

Build your own obstruent-cluster table from natural or elicited SPK001 speech where possible.

### Caution

If only the abstract is accessible, use it only for the claims explicitly present there. Do not pretend to have evaluated the paper's full argument or evidence.

# Week 6 — Syllables and weight

## Rajaa Aquil (2012)
**“The Role of the Syllable in the Segmentation of Cairene Spoken Arabic.”** _Journal of Psycholinguistic Research_ 41(2): 141–158.

PubMed record:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22072294/

### Why it belongs here

The article discusses light, heavy, and superheavy syllables in Cairene Arabic and reports psycholinguistic evidence related to syllable-based segmentation.

### Before reading

From your own corpus:

- propose allowable syllable shapes;
- annotate at least 30–50 words/phrases for syllabification;
- note ambiguous cases;
- state preliminary weight/stress generalizations;
- identify examples that strain the analysis.

### After reading

Separate three questions:

1. What syllable structures does the source posit?
2. What evidence comes from linguistic description?
3. What evidence comes from psycholinguistic segmentation behavior?

Do not treat a processing result as automatically proving a particular abstract phonological representation.

# Week 7 — Word stress and prosodic domains

## Sam Hellmuth (2001)
**“Representation of quantity-sensitivity in a CV-only framework: a study of Cairo Arabic word-stress.”** _SOAS Working Papers in Linguistics_ 11: 95–122.

SOAS Working Papers index:
https://www.soas.ac.uk/about-soas/colleges-schools-and-departments/school-languages-cultures-and-linguistics/department-2

### Use

Read after creating a corpus-based preliminary stress/weight analysis.

Focus on:
- quantity sensitivity;
- domain assumptions;
- relation between syllable weight and stress;
- how much of the analysis depends on the author's representational framework.

## Sam Hellmuth (2006)
**_Intonational pitch accent distribution in Egyptian Arabic._** PhD thesis, SOAS University of London.

Open repository record/PDF:
https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/output/413234/intonational-pitch-accent-distribution-in-egyptian-arabic

### Role

Optional extension for distinguishing:

- lexical word stress;
- postlexical pitch accent;
- prosodic words/phrases;
- intonational organization.

Do not collapse “stress” and “pitch accent” into one phenomenon.

# Week 10 — Morphophonemics and morphology-bounded phonotactics

## Lily Xu (2025)
**“Non-concatenative morphological domains constrain phonotactics: a case study of Egyptian Arabic.”** _Phonology_ 42, e16.

Open-access article:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/phonology/article/nonconcatenative-morphological-domains-constrain-phonotactics-a-case-study-of-egyptian-arabic/D7A17387AF679F619C308722DD1D75E7

### Why this is a capstone reading

The paper examines Egyptian Arabic verb vowel alternation and argues that phonotactic restrictions on consonant–vowel co-occurrence are constrained by morphological domains. The evidence includes a lexicon study and wug test.

This is ideal for the point where AL4303 meets the future AL4410 grammar course.

### Before reading

Build a small paradigm-oriented dataset from SPK001 where feasible:

- related verb forms;
- vowel alternations;
- consonantal root information;
- candidate phonological restrictions;
- candidate morphological conditioning.

Do not attempt to recreate Xu's exact study from memory or secondary summaries.

### After reading

Write a structured comparison:

1. What is the observed alternation?
2. What generalization is phonotactic?
3. What domain is morphological?
4. What evidence comes from the lexicon?
5. What evidence comes from novel-form behavior?
6. What would an AI system need to represent in order to learn this pattern rather than merely imitate observed strings?

This memo becomes a handoff artifact for AL4410.

# Additional literature discovery categories

As the course progresses, add high-quality sources under these headings only when actual data create the need:

- consonant inventory/contrast;
- vowel inventory and quantity;
- emphasis/pharyngealization;
- assimilation;
- syllable structure;
- stress;
- intonation;
- clitic/prosodic domains;
- morphophonemics;
- sociophonetic/sociolectal variation.

Do not collect papers merely because they are about Egyptian Arabic.

---

# Variety caution

“Egyptian Arabic,” “Cairene Arabic,” “educated Cairene Arabic,” and the individual speech of SPK001 are not automatically interchangeable.

Every literature comparison must record, when available:

- location;
- speaker population;
- age/social/educational grouping if relevant;
- elicited vs. natural speech;
- publication date;
- whether the phenomenon is reported as stable, variable, or changing.

A disagreement between your consultant and a paper may reflect variety, sociolect, style, generation, or methodology rather than analytical error.
