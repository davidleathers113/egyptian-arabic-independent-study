# AL4303 Researched Bibliography

This bibliography records the sources chosen for the independent study and the **specific role** each one plays. Inclusion does not mean every source is read cover-to-cover.

## A. DIU scope authority

### Dallas International University — AL4303 Principles of Phonological Analysis
Official distance-course description:
https://www.diu.edu/academics/online-courses/

Use as the authoritative scope boundary for this independent study. DIU's public description includes etic/emic distinction, distinctive features, natural classes, phonetic plausibility, complementary distribution, free variation, contrast, processes, conditioning environments, typological universals, tone analysis, morphophonemics, and orthography preparation.

### Dallas International University — AL5304 Advanced Phonological Analysis
Same course listing page.

Use only as a boundary marker: AL4303 should prepare for, not recreate, the later graduate course's deeper engagement with multiple theories including Optimality Theory and advanced morphophonemics.

# B. Core textbooks

## Donald A. Burquest — _Phonological Analysis_, 3rd ed.

**Role:** primary field-analysis backbone.

Current 2025 SIL release/eBook bibliographic record:
https://books.google.com/books?id=2xVTEQAAQBAJ

SIL archive record for the 3rd-edition chapter structure:
https://pnglanguages.sil.org/resources/archives/438

Why selected:
- explicitly intended for upper-division introductory phonology;
- procedural movement from raw phonetic data to phonemic analysis;
- dedicated morphophonemics chapter;
- natural processes and conditioning domains;
- syllable/larger-unit analysis;
- introductory autosegmental and metrical phonology;
- end-of-chapter problems.

Use systematically across Weeks 1–12.

## Carlos Gussenhoven & Haike Jacobs — _Understanding Phonology_, 5th ed.

**Role:** modern secondary conceptual/typological reference.

Publisher:
https://www.routledge.com/Understanding-Phonology/Gussenhoven-Jacobs/p/book/9781032498416

Why selected:
- current 5th edition;
- broad worldwide language examples including Arabic;
- >100 exercises;
- chapters on underlying forms, distinctive features, syllables/moras, tone, stress, prosodic structure;
- exposes the learner to serial-rule and constraint perspectives without making theoretical mastery the center of AL4303.

Read selectively rather than cover-to-cover.

## Keith L. Snider — _Tone Analysis for Field Linguists_

**Role:** dedicated field-methodology source for Week 8 tone practicum.

2018 bibliographic/description record:
https://books.google.com/books/about/Tone_Analysis_for_Field_Linguistics.html?id=ELkrswEACAAJ

Author/faculty profile noting later editions/publications:
https://www.canil.ca/wordpress/u_member/keith-snider/

Core assigned material:
- Introduction;
- Methodology for Phonological Analysis of Tone;
- selected Phonetics of Pitch material.

Why selected:
- focuses on methodology rather than merely cataloging tone systems;
- emphasizes keeping potentially tone-affecting variables constant;
- provides a bridge from phonetic pitch to phonological tone;
- includes orthography implications;
- designed for field linguists.

# D. Egyptian/Cairene Arabic post-analysis scholarship

These readings are subject to the course's analysis-first embargo.

## Islam Youssef (2016) — “Palatalization in educated Cairene Arabic”

Open article:
https://septentrio.uit.no/index.php/nordlyd/article/view/3739

**Use:** Week 5, after independent process analysis.

Why useful: explicitly distinguishes a phonetic coarticulatory effect from phonological assimilation and discusses possible phonologization across sociolects.

## Rajaa Aquil (2012) — “The Role of the Syllable in the Segmentation of Cairene Spoken Arabic”

Use: Week 6 after independent syllable/phonotactic hypotheses are frozen.

Bibliographic discovery/search through PubMed or institutional access; keep the citation details in the research note when retrieved.

**Use:** compare your syllable-weight and segmentation analysis to published discussion.

## Sam Hellmuth (2001) — “Representation of quantity-sensitivity in a CV-only framework: a study of Cairo Arabic word-stress”

SOAS Working Papers index:
https://www.soas.ac.uk/about-soas/colleges-schools-and-departments/school-languages-cultures-and-linguistics/department-2

**Use:** Week 7 after independent stress analysis.

## Sam Hellmuth — work on Egyptian Arabic intonation/pitch accent

**Use:** optional Week 7 extension. Keep word stress and intonational phonology conceptually separate.

## Lily Xu (2025) — “Non-concatenative morphological domains constrain phonotactics: a case study of Egyptian Arabic”

Open-access article:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/phonology/article/nonconcatenative-morphological-domains-constrain-phonotactics-a-case-study-of-egyptian-arabic/D7A17387AF679F619C308722DD1D75E7

**Use:** Week 10 after independent morphophonemic work.

Why useful:
- morphology–phonology interface;
- Egyptian Arabic verb vowel alternation;
- gradient phonotactic restrictions;
- comparison of morphological domains;
- lexicon-study and wug-test evidence.

## Rawiah Kabrah — work on regressive voicing assimilation in Cairene Arabic

**Use:** optional Week 5 process-analysis comparison if a legitimate copy is accessible.

Do not rely on an abstract alone for detailed analytical claims.

# E. Orthography module

## Michael Cahill & Keren Rice, eds. (2014) — _Developing Orthographies for Unwritten Languages_

SIL archive/TOC:
https://togo-benin.sil.org/resources/archives/56046

**Role:** prevents the orthography memo from assuming that phonemicity alone determines writing-system quality.

### Michael Cahill — “Non-linguistic factors in orthographies”
https://togo-benin.sil.org/resources/archives/56610

Use: required Week 11 reading. Focus on acceptability, usability, policy, education, sociolinguistics, and practical constraints.

### Keith L. Snider — “Orthography and phonological depth”
https://togo-benin.sil.org/resources/archives/56613

Use: required Week 11 reading. Focus on the choice of phonological level represented orthographically and why a simple shallow/deep dichotomy can be inadequate.

### Constance Kutsch Lojenga — “Orthography and tone”
https://mexico.sil.org/resources/archives/56608

Use: optional if the decision memo concerns a tone-language case.

### Constance Kutsch Lojenga — “Basic principles for establishing word boundaries”
https://togo-benin.sil.org/resources/archives/56612

Use: optional if word-boundary decisions are part of the selected orthographic issue.

# Source-handling rules

1. Prefer publisher, university, journal, or author/institutional records.
2. Do not upload copyrighted books or solution manuals to this public repository.
3. For open materials, preserve the canonical source link and license information when relevant.
4. For a paywalled source, do not infer detailed content from an abstract beyond what the abstract actually supports.
5. If a source's edition differs from the edition in hand, record actual page ranges locally rather than copying page numbers from another edition.
6. Egyptian Arabic sources are comparison material after independent analysis, not pre-analysis reference grammars.
