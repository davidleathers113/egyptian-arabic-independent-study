# AL4303 Detailed Reading and Resource Plan

## How to use this plan

This is not a mandate to read every page of every phonology book. Each source has a defined job:

- **Burquest** teaches a field-oriented analytical procedure and supplies the primary problems.
- **Gussenhoven & Jacobs** supplies a modern conceptual map and broader typological/theoretical perspective.
- **Essentials of Linguistics** supplies free review and low-stakes self-checking.
- **Snider** fills the tone-analysis gap that Egyptian Arabic cannot supply.
- **MIT OCW** supplies graduate-level stretch lectures/problems after core competence is established.
- **Egyptian Arabic scholarship** is read only after the learner's relevant analysis is frozen.
- **Orthography readings** keep Week 11 from equating phonemic analysis with orthography design.

Do not invent page numbers across editions. Section/chapter numbers below are stable targets; record actual page ranges from the copies in hand before starting the course.

---

## Primary text

### Donald A. Burquest — _Phonological Analysis_, 3rd edition

Prefer the current SIL International 2025 release where available.

Core chapter architecture:

1. Preliminaries to Analysis
2. Phonemic Analysis
3. Morphophonemics
4. Conditioning by Surrounding Segments
5. Conditioning by Syllable Structure
6. Conditioning by Larger Units
7. Autosegmental Phonology
8. Metrical Phonology

Use the problems at the ends of relevant chapters as the primary recurring analytical practice.

---

## Modern secondary text

### Carlos Gussenhoven & Haike Jacobs — _Understanding Phonology_, 5th ed.

Use selectively:

- Ch. 1 — linguistic structure/evidence
- Ch. 3 — typology, universals, system gaps
- Ch. 4 — allophonic variation, morpheme alternants, underlying forms
- Ch. 5 — distinctive features
- Ch. 6 — serial rules vs. constraints; **orientation only**
- Ch. 7 — lexical/postlexical phonology and phonetic implementation
- Ch. 8 — syllables, sonority, autosegments, moras
- Ch. 9 — tone
- Ch. 10 — word stress
- Ch. 11 — phonology above the word

Chapter 2 is mostly AL4302 review; consult only where needed.

# Week-by-week assignments

## Week 1 — From phones to phonological categories

### Required
- Burquest 1.1–1.2 — place of phonology; foundational principles.
- Burquest 2.1 — basic concepts in phonemic analysis.
- _Essentials of Linguistics_ 4.1 — phonemes and allophones.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs Ch. 1, especially evidence and linguistic structure.

### Practice
- Complete introductory Burquest phonemic-analysis examples/problems appropriate to these sections.
- Build an `observation → hypothesis → evidence needed` ledger from inherited AL4302 Egyptian Arabic data.

### Research embargo
Do not read a published Egyptian Arabic phoneme inventory this week.

---

## Week 2 — Contrast and phonemic procedure

### Required
- Burquest 2.2–2.5 — procedures, worked application, system universals, phonetically similar segments.
- _Essentials_ 4.3 — contrastive distribution/minimal pairs.
- _Essentials_ 4.5–4.6 — phonemic analysis and a second worked analysis.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs 3.2–3.5 — complexity, universals, gaps, cautions.

### Practice
- Burquest Ch. 2 problems focused on contrast and system building.
- Design consultant elicitation that tests 3–5 candidate Egyptian Arabic contrasts without simply asking for spelling distinctions.

### Optional stretch
MIT OCW lecture/readings on contrast and perceptual distinctiveness **after** the basic analysis is complete.

---

## Week 3 — Complementary distribution, variation, phonotactics

### Required
- Burquest 2.6 — free variation.
- Burquest 2.7 — phonotactics, neutralization, underspecification.
- _Essentials_ 4.2 — phonotactics and natural classes.
- _Essentials_ 4.4 — complementary distribution.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs 4.1–4.6 — allophonic variation, morpheme alternants, underlying forms.

### Practice
- Complete distribution tables for unfamiliar-language data.
- Revisit variants observed in AL4302 and classify only what the evidence warrants.

### Egyptian Arabic reading
Still withhold published analysis of the specific phenomena being tested.

---

## Week 4 — Distinctive features and natural classes

### Required
- Burquest 1.3 — phonological features; revisit in light of actual data.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs Ch. 5 — consonant/vowel features, contrastive vs. redundant features, complex segments.
- Revisit _Essentials_ 4.2 for natural-class formulation.

### Practice
- Build feature matrices for several unfamiliar inventories.
- Express candidate processes in terms of natural classes rather than lists of symbols.

### Stretch
- MIT OCW feature-organization lecture notes 10–12, selectively.
- Do not require mastery of feature geometry/underspecification theory beyond the ability to recognize what problem those representations are solving.

---

## Week 5 — Processes, rules, and phonetic plausibility

### Required
- Burquest 4.1–4.5 — sources of natural processes, assimilation, dissimilation, problems.
- _Essentials_ 4.7–4.9 — rules, derivations, types of rules.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs 6.1–6.2 — serial rule formulation/order.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs 7.5, especially distinguishing phonology from phonetic implementation.

### Practice
- Formulate and test rules/process descriptions from cross-linguistic data.
- Require explicit counterexample search.

### Egyptian Arabic post-analysis reading
After freezing your own analysis of any relevant coronal/palatal behavior, read:

- Islam Youssef, “Palatalization in educated Cairene Arabic” (2016).

Use it specifically to ask: what evidence distinguishes a phonetic coarticulatory effect from a phonological assimilation process?

---

## Week 6 — Syllables, phonotactics, neutralization

### Required
- Burquest 5.1–5.7 — syllable structure, ambiguous segments/sequences, syllable-conditioned processes, length, domain phenomena.
- Revisit Burquest 2.7 for phonotactics/neutralization.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs Ch. 8 — syllabification, sonority, autosegments, moras, syllable-based generalizations.

### Practice
- Cross-linguistic syllable-structure problem sets.
- Build a provisional Egyptian Arabic phonotactic inventory from corpus evidence.

### Stretch
- MIT OCW syllable lecture notes 19–21.
- MIT Problem Set 4, “Italian Stressed Syllables,” only after inspecting the task and confirming it serves the week's learning goals.

### Egyptian Arabic post-analysis reading
After freezing your own syllable hypotheses, consult:

- Rajaa Aquil, “The Role of the Syllable in the Segmentation of Cairene Spoken Arabic” (2012).

### Assessment
Midterm practical at the end of the week.

---

## Week 7 — Stress and larger phonological domains

### Required
- Burquest Ch. 6 — word- and utterance-level conditioning.
- Burquest 8.1–8.5 — introductory metrical concepts, syllables, feet, phonological words, extrametricality.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs Ch. 10 — word stress.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs Ch. 11 — prosodic structure above the word.

### Practice
- Separate acoustic prominence observations inherited from AL4302 from claims about abstract stress organization.
- Analyze at least one non-Arabic stress dataset.

### Stretch
- MIT OCW stress lecture notes 22–25.

### Egyptian Arabic post-analysis reading
After freezing your own stress analysis, consult Sam Hellmuth's work on Cairo Arabic word stress and/or Egyptian Arabic intonational structure.

---

## Week 8 — Tone analysis practicum

Egyptian Arabic cannot satisfy the tone requirement. This week is deliberately non-Arabic.

### Required
- Keith L. Snider, _Tone Analysis for Field Linguists_:
  - Ch. 1 — Introduction.
  - Ch. 2 — Methodology for Phonological Analysis of Tone.
  - selected portions of Ch. 3 — Phonetics of Pitch.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs Ch. 9 — tones.
- Burquest 7.1–7.2 — why autosegmental tiers are useful; introductory level only.

### Free/stretch lectures
- MIT OCW Tone-1: African Languages.
- MIT OCW Tone-2: Asian Languages.

### Practicum
Use a documented tone-language dataset with enough controlled evidence to practice:
- pitch vs. phonological tone;
- comparable environments;
- tone-bearing units/melodies;
- lexical or grammatical contrast;
- basic representation of tonal patterning.

If Snider's official/instructor exercises are legitimately available, they are preferred. Otherwise use a documented academic dataset with provenance.

### Assessment
Complete an independent tone-analysis practical, then correct it after feedback/solution access.

---

## Week 9 — Morphophonemics I: finding alternations

### Required
- Burquest 3.1–3.5 — phonological, lexical, and morphological alternations; representations/rules; analytical procedure.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs 4.4–4.5 — morpheme alternants and underlying forms.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs 7.1–7.4 — lexical/postlexical distinctions as a conceptual aid.

### Practice
- Sort alternations by conditioning type before proposing rules.
- Begin Egyptian Arabic verb/noun alternation dataset from observed/elicited data.
- Complete at least one non-Arabic morphophonemic analysis.

---

## Week 10 — Morphophonemics II: underlying forms and competing analyses

### Required
- Burquest 3.6–3.8 — case studies, underlying representations, problems.
- Gussenhoven & Jacobs 4.5 and 6.2 — underlying forms and serial rule application.

### Practice
- Require at least two plausible analyses before choosing one where the data permit alternatives.
- State what evidence would falsify the preferred analysis.

### Egyptian Arabic post-analysis reading
After freezing the relevant Egyptian Arabic morphophonemic analysis, read:

- Lily Xu, “Non-concatenative morphological domains constrain phonotactics: a case study of Egyptian Arabic” (2025).

Focus on how morphological domains can constrain phonotactic generalizations and on the evidence supplied by lexicon study plus wug testing.

### Project
Complete the 6–10 page morphophonemic case study.

---

## Week 11 — Orthography implications + AI benchmark

### Required orthography readings
- Michael Cahill, “Non-linguistic factors in orthographies” (2014).
- Keith Snider, “Orthography and phonological depth” (2014).
- Consult the introduction of Cahill & Rice, eds., _Developing Orthographies for Unwritten Languages_ (2014) if available.

### Optional depending on chosen issue
- Constance Kutsch Lojenga, “Orthography and tone” for a tonal case.
- Constance Kutsch Lojenga, “Basic principles for establishing word boundaries” if boundary decisions are relevant.

### Assignment
Write a limited **orthography decision memo**, not an alphabet design. It must distinguish:
- phonological evidence;
- readability/learnability questions;
- community preferences and sociopolitical factors;
- technological constraints;
- information that cannot be determined without stakeholder research.

### AI benchmark
Only after human grading, submit selected unseen datasets to AI systems and compare:
- detected contrasts;
- proposed representations;
- processes;
- evidential support;
- hallucinations/unsupported certainty;
- alternative analyses missed.

## Week 12 — Synthesis and preparation for graduate work

### Required
- Revisit Burquest chapters most relevant to unresolved analyses.
- Revisit Gussenhoven & Jacobs Ch. 3 and 7 for typology/evidence/implementation distinctions.
- No new large theoretical unit.

### Outputs
- final Egyptian Arabic phonological sketch;
- evidence audit for every major claim;
- unresolved-question/next-elicitation list;
- cross-linguistic breadth index;
- final practical;
- oral defense.

### Optional bridge to later study
Read the description of DIU AL5304 Advanced Phonological Analysis and identify concepts deliberately left for formal graduate-level work, especially deeper theoretical comparison and advanced morphophonemics.

# Reading response standard

For substantial chapters/articles record:

1. full citation;
2. central analytical claim or method;
3. diagnostic/procedure introduced;
4. one worked example reconstructed in your own words;
5. assumptions made by the author;
6. limitation/caution;
7. application to an unfamiliar dataset;
8. possible application to Egyptian Arabic;
9. one question for elicitation or further analysis.

For Egyptian Arabic scholarship add:

10. what your frozen pre-reading analysis predicted;
11. where the source agrees/disagrees;
12. whether you revised your analysis and why.
