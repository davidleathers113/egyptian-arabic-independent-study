# AL4303 Reading Plan

## Primary backbone

### Donald A. Burquest — *Phonological Analysis: A Functional Approach*, 3rd ed.
Use systematically across the course. Match exact chapter/page assignments to the edition actually owned.

Topic mapping:
- Weeks 1–3: preliminaries and phonemic analysis.
- Weeks 4–6: features, phonemic procedures, phonotactics, natural processes, syllable-conditioned processes.
- Weeks 7–8: larger-unit conditioning, metrical/autosegmental concepts as appropriate.
- Weeks 9–10: morphophonemics and underlying representations.
- Weeks 11–12: revisit relevant chapters as analytical reference.

## Secondary/free references

### SIL Glossary of Linguistic Terms
Use for concise definitions of phoneme, allophone, environment, contrast, complementary distribution, natural class, morphophonemics, and related terminology.

### *Essentials of Linguistics*, 2nd ed.
Use the phonology material for accessible review and alternative explanations, not as the analytical depth standard.

### Cross-linguistic datasets
The course requires problem sets from languages outside Arabic. Prefer datasets from university linguistics courses/textbooks with known provenance and solutions that can be withheld until after completion.

## Egyptian Arabic scholarship

Published descriptions of Egyptian/Cairene Arabic should be collected in `research/egyptian-arabic/`, but they are subject to an **analysis-first rule**:

1. analyze the relevant consultant/corpus data independently;
2. freeze the analysis;
3. consult published descriptions;
4. document agreements/disagreements;
5. revise only with an explicit change log and rationale.

## Reading response standard

For substantial chapters/articles record:
- citation;
- central analytical claim;
- procedure or diagnostic introduced;
- one worked example in your own words;
- limitation/caution;
- application to Egyptian Arabic, if any;
- one question for future elicitation or problem-solving.
