# AL4303 Independent Study — Principles of Phonological Analysis

**Modeled DIU course:** AL4303 Principles of Phonological Analysis  
**Modeled credits:** 3  
**Target workload:** 135 documented hours  
**Recommended sequence:** after AL4302 Phonetics

## Purpose

Move from phonetic observation to disciplined phonological analysis. The course trains the student to distinguish etic data from emic structure, formulate and test hypotheses about contrast and distribution, identify natural classes and processes, analyze tone and morphophonemics, and produce a defensible preliminary phonological sketch.

Egyptian Arabic is the sustained field language, but the course deliberately uses data from typologically diverse languages so Arabic does not become the assumed template for sound systems.

## DIU scope alignment

The independent study is bounded by DIU's public description of AL4303: phonetic vs. phonological data, distinctive features, natural classes, phonetic plausibility, complementary distribution, free variation, contrast in identical/analogous environments, phonological processes, conditioning environments, typological universals, tone analysis, and morphophonemics. DIU also frames the course as useful preparation for orthography development or revision.

## Primary handoff from AL4302

Bring forward:
- verified phonetic corpus and metadata;
- broad/narrow transcriptions;
- uncertain contrasts;
- observed variation;
- candidate processes;
- acoustic measurements where relevant;
- questions intentionally left unresolved during phonetics.

Do **not** treat AL4302 observations as phonological conclusions. AL4303 reopens them as hypotheses.

## Major outputs

1. Weekly cross-linguistic problem sets.
2. Egyptian Arabic contrast/distribution notebook.
3. Distinctive-feature and natural-class analyses.
4. Syllable/phonotactic analysis.
5. Tone-analysis practicum using non-Arabic data.
6. Morphophonemic case study.
7. Orthography decision memo.
8. AI phonology benchmark.
9. Midterm and cumulative final practical.
10. **Preliminary Phonological Sketch of Egyptian Arabic Speech from SPK001**.
