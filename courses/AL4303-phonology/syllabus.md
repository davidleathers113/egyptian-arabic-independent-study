# Syllabus — AL4303 Principles of Phonological Analysis — Egyptian Arabic

## Course purpose

Develop practical skill in discovering and defending the sound system of a language from data rather than from spelling, intuition, or preexisting grammatical claims.

## Workload

- 135 documented hours
- 12 instructional weeks
- target average: 11.25 hours/week
- at least 60% of time should involve active analysis, problem sets, elicitation, hypothesis testing, or assessment

## Core domains

1. Phonetics vs. phonology; etic vs. emic evidence.
2. Contrast in identical and analogous environments.
3. Complementary distribution and free variation.
4. Phonemes, allophones, and underlying representations.
5. Distinctive features and natural classes.
6. Phonological rules/processes and conditioning environments.
7. Phonotactics and syllable structure.
8. Neutralization and distributional complications.
9. Stress and larger-domain phenomena.
10. Tone analysis using cross-linguistic datasets.
11. Morphophonemics.
12. Orthography implications and responsible uncertainty.

## Primary working text

**Donald A. Burquest, _Phonological Analysis: A Functional Approach_, 3rd ed.** Use as the main analytical backbone. The text covers phonemic analysis, morphophonemics, natural processes, syllable-conditioned processes, larger-unit conditioning, autosegmental phonology, and metrical phonology.

## Supporting resources

- SIL Glossary of Linguistic Terms for concise definitions and analytic terminology.
- _Essentials of Linguistics_, 2nd ed., phonology chapter for accessible review.
- Cross-linguistic problem sets from reputable linguistics texts/courses.
- Published descriptions of Egyptian Arabic used **after** independent analysis for comparison, not as answer keys.

## Egyptian Arabic role

Egyptian Arabic supplies longitudinal data from the same consultant/corpus used in AL4302. The learner should continually distinguish:

- observed phone;
- proposed phoneme;
- environment;
- process;
- lexical/morphological evidence;
- confidence level;
- unresolved alternative analysis.

## Assessment model

- problem sets: 25%
- Egyptian Arabic analysis notebook/labs: 15%
- midterm practical: 10%
- tone practicum: 10%
- morphophonemic case study: 10%
- orthography memo: 5%
- AI benchmark: 5%
- final phonological sketch: 15%
- final practical/oral defense: 5%

## Passing/mastery standard

85% overall **and** demonstrated competence in contrast/distribution analysis, process formulation, and morphophonemics. A high average cannot compensate for a major unmastered core domain.
