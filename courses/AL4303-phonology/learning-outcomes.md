# AL4303 Learning Outcomes

By the end of the study, the student will be able to:

1. Explain the difference between phonetic and phonological evidence.
2. Identify contrast in identical and analogous environments.
3. Distinguish complementary distribution from free variation.
4. Propose phoneme/allophone analyses and state the evidence for them.
5. Represent phonological segments using appropriate distinctive features.
6. Identify natural classes from feature evidence.
7. Formulate phonological processes with explicit structural descriptions and conditioning environments.
8. Evaluate the phonetic plausibility and explanatory economy of competing analyses.
9. Analyze basic syllable structure, phonotactics, and distributional restrictions.
10. Recognize neutralization and other cases where surface evidence complicates simple phonemic analysis.
11. Analyze stress or other larger-domain patterns when supported by the data.
12. Complete a basic tone analysis from a language dataset even though Egyptian Arabic is not the primary tone practicum language.
13. Analyze morphophonemic alternations and propose cautious underlying representations.
14. Explain how phonological findings can affect orthography design without assuming a one-phoneme/one-letter solution is always appropriate.
15. Collect additional Egyptian Arabic data specifically designed to discriminate between competing phonological hypotheses.
16. Produce a preliminary phonological sketch that clearly separates evidence, analysis, uncertainty, and questions for future research.
17. Critically evaluate AI-generated phonological analyses against independently derived human analyses.

## Evidence rule

Every claimed mastered outcome should link to at least one artifact: problem set, lab, elicitation session, assessment, project section, or oral defense segment.
