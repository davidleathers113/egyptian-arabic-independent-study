# Academic Integrity and AI Use

## Core rule

For assessed work: **human attempt → documented submission → AI critique if allowed → human revision**.

AI is a tutor, critic, coding assistant, and research object. It is not the student.

## Green: permitted

AI may:
- explain a concept after assigned study;
- create additional ungraded practice problems;
- quiz the student;
- generate flashcards from the student's notes;
- challenge a linguistic hypothesis;
- recommend search terms or public sources;
- critique an essay after a complete human draft exists;
- assist with scripts, spreadsheets, plots, or corpus tooling;
- participate in explicitly labeled AI experiments.

## Yellow: only after a human attempt is frozen

AI may critique:
- IPA transcriptions;
- articulatory descriptions;
- acoustic measurements;
- data interpretation;
- project organization.

The original attempt must be saved before AI feedback is consulted.

## Red: prohibited for graded evidence

AI may not:
- produce first-pass answers to graded problem sets;
- transcribe assessment audio before the student;
- write essays or final reports for submission as the student's work;
- answer midterm/final/oral-defense questions;
- silently rewrite measured data or human transcriptions;
- be used as the authority for whether a native expression is natural.

## Disclosure

Material AI assistance on portfolio work should be logged with:
- date;
- model/tool;
- task;
- prompt purpose;
- what was accepted, rejected, or independently verified.

## Assessments

Baseline, midterm, final practical, and oral defense are completed without generative AI assistance. The assessment specification may be public; answer keys and unseen test items should remain private until the assessment is complete.
