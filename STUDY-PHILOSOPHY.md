# Study Philosophy

## Why do the prerequisites independently?

The project assumes that formal credit is secondary to actual preparation. Even when prior coursework might satisfy a prerequisite administratively, returning to foundational linguistics after many years creates an opportunity to rebuild skill deliberately, create current evidence of academic readiness, and enter later graduate work with less cognitive overhead.

## Design principles

### 1. Retrieval and performance matter more than recognition
Reading a phonetics chapter and recognizing terminology is not mastery. The student should be able to hear, produce, transcribe, analyze, explain, and apply the relevant concepts without depending on a textbook or AI system.

### 2. Every outcome should leave evidence
The portfolio should contain artifacts that an external reviewer could inspect: problem sets, audio-linked transcriptions, Praat measurements, essays, project reports, exams, videos, revisions, and reflection.

### 3. Egyptian Arabic is the field language, not the whole curriculum
Deep work with one living variety creates continuity. Cross-linguistic data prevents the student from confusing features of English or Arabic with universals of human language.

### 4. The native speaker is a language consultant, not a linguistic answer key
The consultant supplies natural language data, intuitions, judgments, and interaction. Linguistic hypotheses remain the student's responsibility and should be tested cautiously.

### 5. Mistakes are evidence
Initial transcriptions and analyses should be preserved. Corrected work should show what changed and why. A portfolio that contains only polished final answers hides the learning process.

### 6. AI should increase rigor, not outsource it
AI may generate extra drills, question assumptions, critique completed work, help automate measurements, and become an experimental object. It should not perform the core graded reasoning before the student has done it.

### 7. The course should be sustainable
The curriculum is intentionally compatible with a one-course-at-a-time pace. Consistent weekly work is preferable to bursts that produce exhaustion and shallow retention.
