# Program Completion Requirements — Four Independent Studies

## Scope

This document defines the final evidence package for the modeled **13-credit / 585-hour** independent-study program.

Completion means more than accumulating hours. Every course must meet its own mastery and artifact requirements, and the program must show cumulative development from phonetic observation through phonological/grammatical analysis to real-world language and culture participation.

## 1. Workload evidence

Required:
- 585+ legitimate documented hours;
- course-by-course totals matching or exceeding 135 / 135 / 180 / 135 hours;
- enough activity detail to distinguish reading, analysis, native-speaker work, project work, assessment, and review;
- no retroactive padding to hit totals.

## 2. Course mastery packets

For each course include:
- final syllabus/version;
- learning-outcome matrix;
- representative weekly work;
- assessment results;
- remediation/retest history;
- major project(s);
- final reflection;
- evidence links.

## 3. Required major artifacts

### AL4302
- baseline/final phonetics comparison;
- selected IPA/transcription labs;
- acoustic analysis artifacts;
- final preliminary field-phonetic description.

### AL4303
- representative cross-linguistic phonology problem sets;
- tone practicum;
- morphophonemic case study;
- orthography memo;
- final preliminary phonological sketch.

### AL4410
- representative morphology/syntax problem sets;
- Egyptian Arabic paradigms/interlinear examples;
- nonlinear morphology case study;
- complex-sentence project;
- final preliminary grammatical sketch.

### AA4305
- baseline/final communication evidence;
- 24+ hours acquisition-lab documentation;
- strategy experiments;
- culture-learning portfolio;
- final language/culture acquisition portfolio and continuation plan.

## 4. Cross-linguistic breadth audit

Demonstrate that AL4303 and AL4410 did not become Arabic-only studies. Provide an index of non-Arabic languages represented in graded datasets and the analytical phenomena practiced.

The goal is not a magic number of languages; it is credible evidence that skills transfer beyond familiar language structures.

## 5. Egyptian Arabic longitudinal dossier

Create one index connecting the four studies:

`phonetic observation → phonological analysis → morphological/syntactic analysis → communicative/cultural participation`

Show examples of questions that changed across courses. For example, a sound alternation observed phonetically may become a phonological/morphophonemic analysis and later affect segmentation or acquisition.

## 6. AI research synthesis

Integrate the four course experiments:
- phonetic/speech analysis;
- phonological inference;
- morphology/syntax induction;
- language/culture coaching.

Final synthesis questions:
1. Which AI tasks were consistently strong?
2. Which failures were predictable?
3. Where did AI hide or expose evidence?
4. Where was native-speaker/community judgment indispensable?
5. Which AI workflows could responsibly accelerate Bible-translation preparation?
6. What safeguards, audit trails, or confidence/evidence requirements would a production tool need?

Target synthesis: 5,000–8,000 words or equivalent publishable report.

## 7. Academic integrity dossier

Include:
- AI-use logs or representative samples;
- statement of how closed-AI assessments were protected;
- explanation of analysis-first procedures;
- disclosure of any material outside assistance.

## 8. Data ethics dossier

Include a public-safe statement covering:
- consent model;
- de-identification;
- raw recording storage;
- public/private boundaries;
- AI data handling;
- any approved public excerpts.

Do not publish the private consent artifacts themselves by default.

## 9. Final reflective statement

Write 3,000–5,000 words answering:
- What can I now do that I could not do before the program?
- Which competencies remain weakest?
- How has my understanding of field linguistics changed?
- How has learning Egyptian Arabic changed my view of analysis vs. participation?
- How has empirical work changed my view of AI-assisted Bible translation?
- What should I learn next at DIU/DTS rather than independently?

## 10. Completion decision

The program is complete only when:
- all four course mastery standards are met;
- core weak domains have been remediated;
- required major artifacts are present;
- total hours are documented honestly;
- final cross-program synthesis is complete.

The final status should be recorded as `COMPLETE`, `COMPLETE WITH DOCUMENTED GAPS`, or `INCOMPLETE — REMEDIATION REQUIRED`, with a short rationale.
