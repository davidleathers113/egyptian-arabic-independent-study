# Sources and Reference Standards

Prefer primary/official sources for claims about DIU requirements, IPA conventions, software behavior, SIL resources, and proficiency frameworks. Secondary sources may supplement them.

## DIU

- DIU Course Index: https://www.diu.edu/course/
- DIU nontraditional/distance course listings: https://www.diu.edu/online-courses/
- DIU MA in Applied Linguistics prerequisites: https://www.diu.edu/graduate/ma-applied-linguistics/

As of 2026-08-09, DIU publicly lists the four prerequisite courses used as the model for this project:
- **AL4302** — 3 undergraduate credits, articulatory/acoustic phonetics.
- **AL4303** — 3 undergraduate credits, phonological analysis including etic/emic distinction, features, natural classes, distribution, processes, tone, and morphophonemics.
- **AL4410** — 4 undergraduate credits, practical morphological and syntactic analysis across many languages.
- **AA4305** — 3 undergraduate credits, learner-directed second language/culture acquisition with a native-speaker lab component.

DIU's MA Applied Linguistics page currently says students must earn B- or above in these prerequisites, while allowing course-by-course petitions to waive them with equivalent coursework. Re-check DIU before treating requirements, delivery modes, or schedules as current.

Relevant later-course boundaries:
- **AL4406 Field Methods and Linguistic Analysis** uses these prerequisites to support elicitation, phonological analysis, mini-lexicon development, and a grammar sketch with a speaker of a non-western language.
- **AL5313 Advanced Grammatical Analysis** explicitly adds syntactic typology, formal syntax, and a simplified Lexical Functional Grammar framework.
- **AL5312 Discourse Analysis** adds discourse structure, information structure, participant reference, salience, and paragraph-level analysis.

## Credit-hour workload

The 45-hours-per-credit planning convention is used as a conservative independent-study design target, not as a claim that DIU will award credit. U.S. Department of Education guidance describes a semester credit hour as work reasonably approximating one hour of instruction plus at least two hours of out-of-class work per week for about 15 weeks, or an equivalent amount of work over another format.

- U.S. Department of Education credit-hour guidance: https://www.ed.gov/laws-and-policy/higher-education-laws-and-policy/program-integrity-information-questions-and-12
